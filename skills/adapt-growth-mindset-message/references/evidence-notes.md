# Evidence notes: adapt-growth-mindset-message

Every source below comes from `docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md` (compiled 2026-10-12). Verification tags are carried over as the dossier gives them; nothing has been upgraded or added from memory. Numbers appear only as the dossier writes them.

## Framework

### Walton & Wilson (2018). Wise interventions: Psychological remedies for social and personal problems [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Review*, 125(5), 617–655. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000115.
- The framework: brief, precise interventions that change a specific meaning people make of themselves or their situation. A growth-mindset lesson targets one meaning, that a poor result shows fixed low ability. The saying-is-believing task and the one-session design follow this logic.
- The dossier records no numbers from this paper.

## Anchor trial and reviews (US)

### Yeager et al. (2019). A national experiment reveals where a growth mindset improves achievement [verified-crossref]
*Nature*, 573, 364–369. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1466-y.
- US national study. About +0.10 GPA among lower achievers (Education Week, secondary source per the report). Effects where peer norms supported the message. This is the source of the lower-achiever targeting and the norms check.

### Sisk, Burgoyne, Sun, Butler & Macnamara (2018). Two meta-analyses [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Science*, 29(4), 549–571. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617739704.
- d = 0.08 (k = 43, N = 57,155). About 0.19 for high-risk students and 0.34 for low-SES students, on limited evidence. The skill quotes 0.08 first and the subgroup figures with their "limited evidence" qualifier.

### Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023). Systematic review and meta-analysis with recommendations for best practices [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Bulletin*, 149(3–4), 133–173. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000352.
- 94% of interventions were confounded. Authors with financial incentives reported larger effects. The dossier summarizes the paper's position as apparent effects attributable to design flaws and bias.

### Burnette et al. (2023). For whom, how, and why might such interventions work? [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Bulletin*, 149(3–4), 174–205. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000368.
- Positive effects for those expected to benefit, with large variation. The companion review to Macnamara & Burgoyne; the two disagree, which is why the skill's replication status is "contested".

### Bettinger, Ludvigsen, Rege, Solli & Yeager (2018). Increasing perseverance in math: Norway [verified-crossref]
*Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 146, 1–15. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2017.11.032.
- Norway field experiment; improved perseverance. WEIRD but non-US. The dossier records no effect size.

## Non-WEIRD trials

### Outes-León, Sánchez & Vakis (2020). The power of believing you can get smarter: Peru [verified-crossref]
World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 9141. https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-9141.
- ¡Expande tu Mente!: a 90-minute session, about $0.20 per pupil, more than 50,000 students. Math gains of 0.14 SD by the World Bank's figure, 0.05 to 0.25 depending on estimator, larger outside Lima; the World Bank summary says about 0.2 SD. The figures conflict; the skill quotes the range and says so (dossier correction 24).
- Practitioner source: World Bank eMBeD Peru brief (report, no URL); existence only.

### Ganimian (2020). Growth-mindset interventions at scale: Argentina [verified-crossref]
*Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis*, 42(3), 417–438. https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373720938041.
- Salta, 202 schools. Null, with small effects ruled out. The at-scale null the skill's evidence statement must always carry.

### Indonesia and South Africa (no results in the dossier)
- An Indonesia replication, "Semua Bisa Pintar!" (about 160,000 students), and a South Africa adaptation exist per the labs report, with no results cited. Do not quote outcomes for either.

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)

### DellaVigna & Linos (2022). RCTs to scale: Comprehensive evidence from two nudge units [verified-crossref]
*Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709.
- 126 RCTs, 23 million people. Academic journal studies average 8.7 percentage points (+33.4%); nudge-unit trials average 1.4 points (+8.0%). About 70% of the gap is explained by selective publication combined with low power.

### Ferraro & Tracy (2022). Loss-framed incentive contracts: meta-analysis and real-effort experiment [verified-crossref]
*Experimental Economics*, 25(5), 1441–1466. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-022-09754-x.
- Pooled 0.16 SD, but 0.33 SD in the lab versus 0.02 SD in the field, with publication bias among lab studies. The dossier's lab-to-field shrinkage example; the skill uses it with DellaVigna & Linos for the "discount published effects" point.

### Bryan, Tipton & Yeager (2021). Behavioural science is unlikely to change the world without a heterogeneity revolution [verified-crossref]
*Nature Human Behaviour*, 5(8), 980–989. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01143-3.
- Expect heterogeneous effects; measure moderators. The dossier notes the fetched text did not discuss WEIRD samples, so the skill attributes no WEIRD claim to it.

## Non-WEIRD evidence, summarized
- Peru (Outes-León et al. 2020): positive, modest, conflicting estimates (0.05 to 0.25 SD).
- Argentina (Ganimian 2020): null, small effects ruled out.
- Indonesia, South Africa: adaptations exist, no results cited.

## Replication status
- Contested. Pooled d = 0.08 across k = 43, N = 57,155; the two 2023 reviews disagree on whether subgroup effects survive design corrections; one LMIC trial modestly positive with conflicting estimates, one null at scale.

## Known corrections and cautions (from the dossier)
- Peru: quote the 0.05 to 0.25 range alongside 0.14 and "about 0.2"; the figures conflict (correction 24).
- The Yeager 2019 GPA figure and the World Bank Peru summary figure are secondary sources per the report.
- Indonesia and South Africa outcomes are not in the dossier.
- The dossier lists no practitioner source with a URL for this skill.

## What to check locally
- Whether students read a poor result as a verdict on fixed ability, in their own words; without that, the entry condition is not met.
- Whether teachers give feedback or a second attempt after a poor result, or sort students; the lesson is false where there is no occasion to improve.
- What peers say about trying hard; the US effect appeared only where peer norms supported it.
- Whether examinations are one-shot and streaming is early, which can make "ability grows" a claim the system contradicts.
- Whether the examples of skill growth are recognizable, and whether a written letter task suits the reading level or an oral version is needed.
- Whether grades or test scores can be measured at a named point, with a comparison group, and pre-registered.
