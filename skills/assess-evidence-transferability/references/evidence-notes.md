## Anchor sources

### Szaszi, B., et al. (2025). Generalizability of choice architecture interventions. *Nature Reviews Psychology*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-025-00471-9
- Verification: verified-crossref for first author, year, and title. This is a plan-only citation, not in the three research reports. The plan lists the co-authors as Goldstein, Soman and Michie; the audit did not confirm that list. Unverified, author to confirm.
- What it says (per the plan): a review of when choice architecture interventions generalize. No numbers recorded.
- How this skill uses it: the framing that generalizability is a question to ask per intervention, not a property to assume.

### Henrich, J., Heine, S. J., & Norenzayan, A. (2010). The weirdest people in the world? *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 33, 61. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999152X
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- What it says: most behavioral-science samples are Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic, and unusual on many measures. The dossier instructs that the WEIRD argument be attributed here.
- How this skill uses it: the units row of the fit table; a study's population is a condition of its result.

### Bryan, C. J., Tipton, E., & Yeager, D. S. (2021). Behavioural science is unlikely to change the world without a heterogeneity revolution. *Nature Human Behaviour*, 5(8), 980–989. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01143-3
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- What it says: expect heterogeneous effects; avoid "haphazard convenience samples"; use probability samples with moderators measured. The fetched text did not explicitly discuss WEIRD samples; do not attribute a WEIRD claim to it.
- How this skill uses it: the expectation that an effect varies across people and settings, which is why a single-setting finding gets a wider discount.

### DellaVigna, S., & Linos, E. (2022). RCTs to scale: Comprehensive evidence from two nudge units. *Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- Numbers: 126 RCTs, 23 million people. Academic journal studies average 8.7 percentage points (+33.4%); nudge-unit trials average 1.4 points (+8.0%). About 70% of the gap is explained by selective publication combined with low power. US government units only.
- How this skill uses it: the published-to-field discount in step 5.

### Ferraro, P. J., & Tracy, J. D. (2022). A reassessment of the potential for loss-framed incentive contracts to increase productivity. *Experimental Economics*, 25(5), 1441–1466. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-022-09754-x
- Verification: verified-crossref (DOI located in the audit).
- Numbers: pooled 0.16 SD; 0.33 SD in the lab versus 0.02 SD in the field; publication bias among lab studies.
- How this skill uses it: the lab-to-field shrinkage example; never quote the pooled or lab figure alone.

### Blumenstock, J., Callen, M., & Ghani, T. (2018). Why do defaults affect behavior? Experimental evidence from Afghanistan. *American Economic Review*, 108(10), 2868–2901. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20171676
- Verification: verified-crossref. Working-paper version: NBER w23590, https://doi.org/10.3386/w23590 [verified-crossref].
- Numbers: 949 employees of one firm (Roshan, mobile money). A 5% default contribution versus 0% raised participation by 40 points, equivalent to a 50% employer match. Mechanism: present bias and the cognitive cost of deciding, not endorsement or inattention.
- How this skill uses it: the worked contrast (a non-WEIRD field result with a stated mechanism), and the illustration in step 3 of a setting difference that touches the mechanism.

### Gauri, V., et al. (2018). eMBeDding for impact and scale in developing contexts. *Behavioural Public Policy*, 2(2), 256–262. https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2018.11
- Verification: verified-crossref (DOI located in the audit). The eMBeD cycle itself was seen only in a third-party graphic.
- What it says: proven nudges are not always effective in developing countries (report, verified page, no URL recorded).
- How this skill uses it: practitioner support for not carrying effects across settings.

### Singh, A. (2023). *Before You Wreck Yourself: A Guide to Facing the Hard Truth That We Are All One Level Removed*. Busara and MIT GOV/LAB. https://mitgovlab.org/resources/before-you-wreck-yourself/
- Verification: verified-fetch. 24 experts across 22 organizations interviewed September to November 2022.
- How this skill uses it: practitioner source for the habit of checking one's distance from the population before relying on a finding.

## Cross-cutting cautions that may be quoted alongside the discount
- Mertens et al. (2022), *PNAS*, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2107346118 [verified-crossref]: d = 0.45 (95% CI 0.39–0.52) across more than 200 studies, more than 450 effect sizes, N about 2.15 million; moderate publication bias. Some secondary sources say 0.43.
- Maier et al. (2022), *PNAS*, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2200300119 [verified-crossref]: bias-corrected average about d = 0.04; the average hides heterogeneity.
- Szaszi et al. (2022), *PNAS* letter, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2200732119 [verified-crossref]: bias-adjusted range d about 0.03–0.31.
Never quote the uncorrected 0.45 without the corrected figures beside it.

## Non-WEIRD evidence
Afghanistan (Blumenstock et al. 2018) as the worked contrast; eMBeD's statement that proven nudges are not always effective in developing countries.

## Replication status
Conceptual: the skill is a lens. The shrinkage it relies on appears in two independent comparisons (DellaVigna and Linos; Ferraro and Tracy). Blumenstock et al. is a single-firm study.

## Boundary conditions from the dossier
Compare sample and target on culture, institutions, literacy, and infrastructure; check for LMIC replications; flag assumptions (labs report procedure). This skill probes one study's fit on units, treatments, outcomes, settings, and implementer; it does not do cultural theory, and its "Do not use when" says so.

## What this skill adds beyond the sources
- The five-dimension fit table with a "touches the mechanism?" column.
- The rule that only mechanism-relevant differences change the verdict.
- A discount expressed as a provisional range that starts from the published-to-field gap.
- "Cannot tell" as a legitimate verdict.
These are drafting rules for this library, not published criteria, and the reviewer may change them.

## What to check locally
- Whether the study's claimed mechanism is present in the target population (for a default: is there a payroll or wallet deduction to default into, and is the opt-out a real choice?).
- Whether the outcome the study measured is the outcome the program cares about (participation is not retention).
- Whether the implementer's relationship to the population resembles the study's (trust, authority, capacity).
- Whether any near-setting replication exists in the evidence scan brief; if none, treat the finding as single-setting.
