# Evidence notes: audit-intervention-ethics

Annotated bibliography for the skill. Every entry is copied from the library's evidence dossier (`docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md`, entry `audit-intervention-ethics`, plus sections A and B) with the dossier's verification tag. Nothing was added from memory; a number appears only as the dossier writes it. "Unverified, author to confirm" means neither a Crossref nor a fetch check succeeded, and the item must not be cited as established. The sources are normative: they state principles, not effects, so there are no effect sizes, samples, or replications to record.

## Anchor sources

**OECD (2022).** *Good practice principles for ethical behavioural science in public policy*. OECD Public Governance Policy Papers. https://doi.org/10.1787/e19a9be9-en [verified-crossref] (DOI located in the audit; published 5 October 2022 per the labs report; the OECD web page returned 403, so the DOI is the reference)
- The public-policy statement of principles this skill reviews against. The dossier records its title and existence only, not its text; the skill's six tests are the drafter's mapping and must be checked against the published document (unverified, author to confirm).

**Sunstein, C. R. (2015).** The ethics of nudging. *Yale Journal on Regulation*, 32(2), 413. [verified-fetch: https://www.yalejreg.com/print/the-ethics-of-nudging/] (the page gives volume 32, issue 2; year and starting page from the PDF filename). SSRN version (2014): https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2526341 [verified-crossref]
- The source of the autonomy, welfare, and dignity tests, with transparency as the condition that lets them be checked. The dossier lists these four words against this paper; the skill's phrasing of each test is its own.

**OECD (2019).** *Tools and Ethics for Applied Behavioural Insights: The BASIC Toolkit*. https://doi.org/10.1787/9ea76a8f-en [verified-crossref] (Crossref title "OECD Behavioural Insights Toolkit and Ethical Framework"; no author field; developed with Pelle Guldborg Hansen per the labs report; the full report returned 403 in the research session)
- Cited for its ethics section, which the dossier names without quoting. The "ABCD" sub-tool mentioned in the labs report is unverified and is not used.

**Lades, L. K., & Delaney, L. (2022).** Nudge FORGOOD. *Behavioural Public Policy*, 6(1). https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2019.53 [verified-crossref] (audit year 2020, online-first; plan-only citation)
- A named ethics framework for nudging. The dossier gives the acronym and the title only and instructs the drafter not to expand the acronym into dimensions without reading the paper. The skill cites it as a related framework and takes nothing from it.

**Mathur, A., et al. (2019).** Dark patterns at scale. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction*, 3(CSCW), 81. https://doi.org/10.1145/3359183 [verified-crossref]
- 15 dark-pattern types in 7 categories from about 11,000 shopping websites (labs report). The dossier records the counts, not the type names; the skill uses the paper as the "would a reviewer call this a dark pattern" test for interface-based interventions.

**Gray, C. M., et al. (2018).** The dark (patterns) side of UX design. *CHI 2018*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174108 [verified-crossref]
- 5 categories from 118 practitioner examples (labs report). Same use as Mathur et al.; type names not recorded in the dossier and not quoted.

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)

- **Mertens, S., et al. (2022).** PNAS, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2107346118 [verified-crossref]: d = 0.45 (95% CI 0.39–0.52), more than 200 studies, N about 2.15 million, moderate publication bias; "structure" interventions such as defaults beat "description" and "assistance".
- **Maier, M., et al. (2022).** No evidence for nudging after adjusting for publication bias. PNAS, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2200300119 [verified-crossref]: bias-corrected average about d = 0.04; a rejoinder notes the average hides heterogeneity.
- **DellaVigna, S., & Linos, E. (2022).** RCTs to scale. *Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709 [verified-crossref]: 126 RCTs, 23 million people; academic journal studies average 8.7 percentage points (+33.4%), nudge-unit trials 1.4 points (+8.0%); about 70% of the gap is selective publication plus low power.
- Why they are here: the welfare test weighs a benefit against a cost. If the benefit is the published effect, it is usually overstated; the audit discounts it before weighing.

## What to check locally

- Who gives consent for whom: the person, a household head, a village assembly, or a parent; and whether individual opt-out is realistic where decisions are collective.
- Whether public visibility of a behavior reads as recognition or as shaming here, and what the sanctions against non-doers are (social, economic, violent).
- Whether the deliverer (government, NGO, employer, lender, chief, teacher) holds power over the audience, which turns an opt-out on paper into no opt-out in practice.
- Which data are already public in this setting (a village list of who built a latrine may be ordinary) and which are sensitive (HIV status, debt, pregnancy, migration status).
- Whether children are in the audience at all, including as messengers or monitors, and what the organization's safeguarding policy requires.
- Whether a research ethics board or institutional review board must approve the intervention, the evaluation, or both, and how early it must see the draft.
