# Worked example: a community-led sanitation pledge in Indonesia

Illustrative run on an invented program. Names, counts, and details are made up to show the skill's shape; none is a finding. The concern that community-led total sanitation (CLTS) programs have used shaming tactics is named in the library's dossier without a verified paper (unverified, author to confirm); this example shows how the audit handles a draft of that kind, not whether any real program did this.

**Setting.** "Desa Sehat" (illustrative), a sanitation program in 30 villages of a district in East Java, Indonesia, run by a local NGO with the district health office. The draft came from `plan-collective-pledge`.

**Inputs supplied.** Draft: in each village a facilitated "triggering" meeting walks residents past open-defecation sites, then draws a village map on which each household without a latrine is marked by the household head's name; the village head sets a date by which the village will declare itself open-defecation free; households sign a pledge sheet at the meeting; a children's group is formed to whistle at adults seen defecating in the open in the following weeks; the declaration is celebrated with a sign at the village entrance. Mechanism: a collective pledge with public visibility of non-doers. Deliverer: NGO facilitators with the village head. Audience: all households; children take part as monitors. Told: that the village is choosing to end open defecation. Not told: that the named map will stay on the meeting-hall wall. Data: the named map; a list of pledge signatories kept by the village head. Outcome evidence: the program cites coverage gains from CLTS elsewhere (the program's own claim; the dossier's figures sit under `plan-collective-pledge`).

```markdown
# Intervention ethics audit

**Intervention in one paragraph:** NGO facilitators and the village head hold a meeting at which residents walk past open-defecation sites, see a map naming each household without a latrine, sign a pledge to end open defecation by a date the head sets, and form a children's group to whistle at adults seen in the open; the village then declares itself open-defecation free at a ceremony. It works by making the practice, and who still does it, visible to everyone.
**Mechanism:** collective pledge with public visibility of non-doers (shame)
**Deliverer and audience:** NGO and village head to every household in the village **Children or dependents in the audience:** yes: children are enlisted as monitors; renters and the poorest households depend on the head for land and aid lists

## Tests
| Test | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Transparency | concern | The pledge and the date can be disclosed and the effect survives; the named map and the children's monitoring were not going to be announced in advance, and their effect depends on surprise and exposure |
| Autonomy | concern | A household can decline to sign, but in front of the head and neighbors, with its name already on the map; declining is visible, and the head controls aid lists |
| Welfare | concern | Latrine coverage serves households' own health; the cost falls on households that cannot afford a latrine, who gain exposure without the means to comply; the expected coverage effect should be discounted from headline figures |
| Fairness and equity | fail | The poorest, renters, and the elderly are the households most likely to be named and least able to build; the design concentrates exposure on them |
| Dignity | fail | Naming households on a wall map and sending children to whistle at adults are shaming by design; the people exposed did not choose the visibility |
| Consent and data | concern | Residents are told the village is choosing to end open defecation; they are not told the named map stays on the wall; the pledge list is held by the head with no stated use or end date |

## Flags
- **Coercion:** social sanction by design (whistling, the named map); no formal penalty, but the head's control of aid lists makes informal penalty plausible
- **Restriction:** none
- **Incentives to people who cannot refuse:** none offered; the absence of any support for households that cannot build is the equity problem above
- **Children:** yes: enlisted to police adults, which exposes them to conflict and teaches them to shame

## Verdict: REVISE
Two rows fail and both are fixed by removing the shaming components while keeping the collective pledge. The named map and the children's monitoring are the parts that work by exposure; the walk, the pledge, the date, and the ceremony work by shared commitment and can stay. If the program insists on the named map or the children's role, the verdict is stop.

## Required changes before launch
1. Replace the named household map with an aggregate count ("23 of 140 households have no latrine", illustrative) and keep any household-level list private to the health worker (to be made in `plan-collective-pledge`)
2. Drop the children's whistling group; if monitoring is kept, adults volunteer for it and report counts, not names (`plan-collective-pledge`)
3. Say at the meeting that signing is voluntary, let households sign privately after the meeting, and keep the signatory list with the health worker rather than the head (`plan-collective-pledge`)
4. Add a route for households that cannot afford a latrine before the date is set, such as a labor-sharing arrangement or a subsidy, and audit that route separately (`design-incentive-scheme`)
5. State what residents are told in advance, including who holds any list and for how long (`plan-collective-pledge`)

## Questions for the organization's ethics board
- If the 30 villages are to be compared in an evaluation, consent to be researched and the handling of household-level data are the board's questions
- Whether any involvement of children in the program, even as participants in the ceremony, needs the board's or a safeguarding lead's review under the organization's policy

## Open questions
- Whether the village head's control of aid lists in these villages makes any visible declining unsafe; a local qualitative researcher can answer this before the revised draft is audited again
- The actual share of households that cannot afford a latrine, which sets how large the equity problem is
```

**What happened next (illustrative).** The draft went back to `plan-collective-pledge` with the five changes; the support route went to `design-incentive-scheme`; the revised draft is to be audited again before launch. No results are reported here.
