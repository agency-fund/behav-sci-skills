## Skill details

### besci-eval

**Title:** Behavioral science skill evaluator  
**Stage:** Other · **Type:** meta · **Status:** draft · **Version:** 0.1.0 · **Org:** TAF  
**Tags:** meta, evaluation, regression  
**Consumes:** skill-draft  
**Produces:** eval-report  

**What it does:** Runs three checks on one library skill: structure, triggering, output quality.

**Description (trigger rule):** Use when someone wants to check how good a skill in the Behavioral Science Skills Library is: validate its structure, test whether it fires on the right requests and stays quiet on the wrong ones, grade its outputs against its own template, compare it across models, or re-test it after a new model is released. Trigger on "evaluate this skill", "test my skill", "does this skill trigger correctly", "run the evals", "regression test", "compare models on this skill", "is this skill still good on the new model". Do not use for writing or revising a skill's content (besci-skill-creator), for choosing which skill to use (besci-navigator), or for evaluating an AI product or chatbot (that is The Agency Fund's evaluation playbook, a different thing).

**Use it when:**
- "Evaluate my skill before I submit it"
- "Run the evals for decompose-comb-barriers"
- "Does this skill trigger on the right prompts? I think it over-fires."
- "A new Claude model came out; which of our skills got worse?"
- "Compare this skill on Opus and Sonnet"
- "Grade these three outputs against the skill's template"
- A maintainer reviewing a pull request wants an independent read of the evals

**Do not use it when:**
- The author wants to change the skill's content, add sections, or rewrite the description; use besci-skill-creator, then come back
- The user wants to know which skill to use for their problem; use besci-navigator
- The user wants to evaluate an AI chatbot, product, or program outcome; that is The Agency Fund's four-level evaluation framework at https://eval.playbook.org.ai/, not skill evaluation
- The skill is not a library skill (no eight sections, no evals.json); run the structural check only and say the rest does not apply

**When to bring in a specialist:**

Tell the author or maintainer to involve a domain specialist when:
- Output-quality grading finds that two runs give substantively different method advice, since the evaluator can judge shape but not which advice is right.
- The skill's evidence base cannot be verified from the cited sources during grading.
- The skill touches clinical, safety, or child-protection content; the rubric's escalation criterion is necessary but not sufficient there.
- A model comparison shows a quality drop on the new model and the author cannot tell whether the skill or the model is at fault.

Bring them: the report, the two most different outputs for the same case, and the skill's "What it draws on" section.

### besci-navigator

**Title:** Behavioral science navigator  
**Stage:** Other · **Type:** meta · **Status:** draft · **Version:** 0.1.0 · **Org:** TAF  
**Tags:** meta, navigation, workflow  
**Consumes:** program-goal  
**Produces:** workflow-draft  

**What it does:** Recommends which library skills to run, in what order, for the problem a user describes.

**Description (trigger rule):** Use when someone describes a program, product, research, or measurement problem in plain words and does not know which behavioral-science method or library skill to use. Trigger on "where do I start", "which skill should I use", "what should I do first", "help me plan this", "what does the library have for X", and on any description of uptake, adherence, retention, dropout, or behavior-change problems that names no method. Recommends which skills to run, in what order, or drafts a workflow. Do not use when the user names a specific method or skill; let that skill fire. Not for creating skills (besci-skill-creator) or evaluating them (besci-eval).

**Use it when:**
- "Mothers stop coming to our nutrition sessions after the third visit. Where do I start?"
- "We want to improve uptake of the new savings product. What should we do first?"
- "I need to measure agency for our baseline but I don't know which approach fits."
- "Which skill should I use to pretest our survey?"
- "What does the library have for designing an evaluation?"
- "Walk me through planning a behavioral intervention for farmer adoption of X."
- The user describes a problem with people not doing something and names no framework

**Do not use it when:**
- The user names a specific method or skill ("run COM-B on this", "draft a cognitive interview script"); that skill should fire directly
- The user wants to build a new skill; use besci-skill-creator
- The user wants to test or score a skill; use besci-eval
- The user wants behavioral-science advice with no intention of using a skill; answer as a thought partner and mention the library only if a skill genuinely fits

**When to bring in a specialist:**

Recommend a behavioral scientist, and say at which step, when:
- The target behavior involves clinical, safety, legal, or child-protection consequences.
- The user has no one to review the design before it reaches participants and the intervention will reach more than a pilot's worth of people.
- Two or more recommended skills have escalation points that the situation triggers.
- The user is adapting a method far outside the contexts its evidence comes from and cannot validate locally.
- The user asks for a full program design: the workflow can structure the work, but the judgement calls between steps are where expertise matters most.

Bring them: the workflow draft, the catalog entries for the recommended skills, and the answers to the guide-mode questions. For organizations looking for ongoing support, point to Find My BeSci and to hiring guidance from the partnership.

### besci-skill-creator

**Title:** Behavioral science skill creator  
**Stage:** Other · **Type:** meta · **Status:** draft · **Version:** 0.1.0 · **Org:** TAF  
**Tags:** meta, authoring  
**Produces:** skill-draft  

**What it does:** Interviews an author about one behavioral-science method until every part of a library skill is clear, then drafts the complete skill folder.

**Description (trigger rule):** Use when someone wants to create a new skill for the Behavioral Science Skills Library, turn a method, framework, paper, playbook, or checklist into a skill, revise an existing library skill, or check a draft skill against the library's spec. Trigger on "create a besci skill", "turn this method into a skill", "write a SKILL.md for the behavioral science library", "contribute a skill", "is my skill atomic", "check my skill draft". Do not use for skills outside this library (Anthropic's skill-creator handles general skills). Not for evaluating a finished skill across models (besci-eval) or for finding which skill to use (besci-navigator).

**Use it when:**
- "I want to turn the saying-is-believing technique into a skill for the library"
- "Help me write a SKILL.md for COM-B barrier decomposition"
- "Here's a playbook chapter; make it a besci skill"
- "Is this idea atomic enough to be one skill?"
- "Check my draft skill against the library spec"
- "Revise my skill; the reviewer said the trigger rule is too vague"
- "Contribute a skill" or "claim a skill" for the behav-sci-skills repo

**Do not use it when:**
- The skill is not for this library (a PDF tool, a coding workflow); use Anthropic's general skill-creator
- The author wants to test a finished skill across models or score its outputs; use besci-eval
- The user wants to know which existing skill fits their problem; use besci-navigator
- The user wants to write a workflow (an ordered set of skills); use the workflow template in the repo, with besci-navigator's guide mode

**When to bring in a specialist:**

Tell the author to bring in a domain expert before submitting when:
- The method has no published evidence base and the author is not themselves a practitioner of it.
- The skill touches clinical, safety, legal, or child-protection decisions, where a wrong procedure causes harm.
- The author cannot answer the WEIRD-skew or replication questions and the skill will be used outside the evidence's context.

Bring them: the one-sentence description, the evidence list, and the "Where it goes wrong" draft. A maintainer from TAF or IL reviews every skill's content before merge regardless.

### draft-cognitive-interview-script

**Title:** Cognitive interview script drafter  
**Stage:** Measure · **Type:** atomic · **Status:** draft · **Version:** 0.1.0 · **Org:** TAF  
**Tags:** measurement, qualitative, survey-design, psychometrics  
**Consumes:** survey-items  
**Produces:** interview-guide  

**What it does:** Turns a set of draft survey items into a cognitive interview script that tests whether respondents interpret each item the way its author intended.

**Description (trigger rule):** Use when a team has draft survey or questionnaire items and wants to check whether respondents understand them as intended before fielding them. Also use when someone says "pretest our survey", "cognitive testing", "do these questions make sense to respondents", or "think-aloud interviews for our questionnaire". Produces a full cognitive interview script with probes per item and a note-taking sheet. Do not use when the goal is to explore a topic or understand lived experience; that is a qualitative interview guide, not item pretesting. Not for psychometric validation with numbers (reliability, factor structure); run a measurement skill after the items survive this one.

**Use it when:**
- A user has draft survey items (a scale, an intake form, an M&E questionnaire) and asks "will people understand these?" or "can you pretest this?"
- A user asks for "cognitive testing", "cognitive interviews", "think-aloud testing", or "verbal probing" for a questionnaire
- A measure is being adapted to a new language, literacy level, or population and the team wants to check it still means the same thing
- A team is about to translate a validated scale and field it without checking comprehension locally
- Field staff report that respondents "don't get" certain questions and the team wants a structured way to find out why

**Do not use it when:**
- The user wants to explore experiences, motivations, or barriers in depth; that is a semi-structured qualitative interview guide, and the questions there are open, not item probes
- The user wants to know whether the items measure the construct reliably (Cronbach's alpha, factor analysis, item response theory); that is quantitative validation and comes after cognitive testing
- The user has no items yet and wants help writing them; draft the items first, then come back
- The "items" are behavioral tasks or games rather than questions; usability testing is the closer method

**When to bring in a specialist:**

Tell the user to consult a survey methodologist or a psychometrician when:
- The items are part of a validated scale whose scoring depends on the exact wording, and cognitive testing suggests changing wording; a specialist can judge what changes break comparability.
- The instrument will be used for high-stakes decisions (eligibility, diagnosis, program exit) and the team cannot run a quantitative validation afterward.
- Problems cluster on sensitive items (violence, mental health, income) in a way that suggests the survey mode itself, not the wording, is the issue.
- The interviews are cross-language and the team has no bilingual reviewer to judge whether a problem is translation or comprehension.

Bring them: the items with intended meanings, the tabulated problem codes, and three verbatim quotes per problematic item.

## Stages

- **define** — Define: Turn a goal, population, or outcome into something specific enough to work on. (bit_test: Target; busara_audas: Assess; taf_eval: Level 0: theory of change)
- **diagnose** — Diagnose: Explain why the current behavior pattern exists: barriers, drivers, context. (bit_test: Explore; busara_audas: Understand; taf_eval: Level 1: formative research)
- **design** — Design: Produce intervention content, levers, messages, or strategy. (bit_test: Solution; busara_audas: Design; taf_eval: Level 1: prototype)
- **measure** — Measure: Choose, adapt, or build instruments for a behavior or construct. (bit_test: Trial (measurement); busara_audas: Assess; taf_eval: Level 2: outcome measures)
- **test** — Test: Plan a pilot, experiment, or evaluation of an intervention. (bit_test: Trial; busara_audas: Assess; taf_eval: Level 3: experimental evaluation)
- **analyze** — Analyze: Analyze data from a test or evaluation and interpret the result. (bit_test: Trial (analysis); busara_audas: Assess; taf_eval: Level 3 and 4: analysis)
- **implement** — Implement: Roll out, adapt, or scale what worked, and report what was done. (bit_test: Scale; busara_audas: Scale; taf_eval: Level 4: scale and welfare)
- **other** — Other: Does not fit the seven stages. The validator warns so maintainers can review the gap.
