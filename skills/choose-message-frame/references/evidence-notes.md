# Evidence notes: choose-message-frame

Every entry below comes from `docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md`: the section C entry for this skill, and section A for the cross-cutting cautions. Tags are the dossier's. `[verified-crossref]` means the DOI resolved and the first author, year, and title match; `[unverified, author to confirm]` means neither check succeeded. Numbers appear only as the dossier gives them; "effect size unverified" is the dossier's own wording.

## Message framing

### Gallagher & Updegraff (2012) [verified-crossref]
Gallagher, K. M., & Updegraff, J. A. (2012). Health message framing effects on attitudes, intentions, and behavior: A meta-analytic review. *Annals of Behavioral Medicine*, 43, 101–116. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-011-9308-7. Online-first 2011; an erratum exists (https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-012-9446-6).
- 94 studies, 189 effect sizes, health messages.
- Gain frames beat loss frames for prevention behavior, r = 0.083.
- No framing effect on attitudes or intentions; no framing effect for detection behavior.
- Population: health-communication studies; the dossier records no country breakdown.

### Karlan, McConnell, Mullainathan & Zinman (2016) [verified-crossref]
*Management Science*, 62(12), 3393–3411. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2296
- Savings reminders in Bolivia, Peru, and the Philippines. Reminders raised savings-goal attainment; messages naming the goal plus the incentive worked best.
- Loss versus gain framing of the reminder made no difference. Effect size unverified.

### Hallsworth, List, Metcalfe & Vlaev (2017) [verified-crossref]
*Journal of Public Economics*, 148, 14–31. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2017.02.003
- UK tax letters, natural field experiments. Norm and public-goods messages raised payment; no loss-frame advantage.

## Loss-framed incentive contracts

### Ferraro & Tracy (2022) [verified-crossref]
*Experimental Economics*, 25(5), 1441–1466. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-022-09754-x
- Meta-analysis plus a real-effort experiment. Pooled 0.16 SD; lab 0.33 SD versus field 0.02 SD; publication bias among lab studies.
- The dossier names this as the library's lab-to-field shrinkage example (section A9).

### Fryer, Levitt, List & Sadoff (2022; working paper 2012) [verified-crossref]
*American Economic Journal: Economic Policy*. https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20190287. Working paper: NBER w18237 (2012), https://doi.org/10.3386/w18237
- Teacher bonuses paid up front and clawed back (loss contract) versus paid at year end.
- 0.20–0.40 SD in the 2012 working paper; revised to 0.124 SD in the 2022 published version; no effect in wave 2. The title changed from "through Loss Aversion" to "through Framing". Quote 0.124 SD.

### Bulte, List & van Soest (2020) [verified-crossref]
*The Economic Journal*. https://doi.org/10.1093/ej/ueaa054. NBER w26286 (title "...in Uganda"), https://doi.org/10.3386/w26286
- About 1,000 peri-urban workers in Uganda. Loss-framed contracts raised productivity at a modest welfare cost; effect size unverified.
- Most workers preferred gain contracts; the productivity gain appeared only among the roughly 20% who chose loss framing.

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)

### DellaVigna & Linos (2022) [verified-crossref]
*Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709
- 126 RCTs, 23 million people. Academic-journal nudges average 8.7 percentage points (+33.4%); nudge-unit trials 1.4 points (+8.0%). About 70% of the gap is selective publication combined with low power.

### Pattern the dossier asks every technique skill to state
Lab-to-field shrinkage in loss framing (0.33 to 0.02 SD) and the academic-versus-unit gap (8.7 vs 1.4 points) point the same way: discount published lab or academic effects when transferring them to a field program.

## Non-WEIRD evidence
- Uganda: Bulte, List & van Soest (2020), as above.
- Bolivia, Peru, Philippines: Karlan et al. (2016), no frame effect.
- No LMIC test of gain-versus-loss message framing for a health or financial behavior is recorded in the dossier.

## Known corrections
- Quote Fryer et al. at 0.124 SD (published), not 0.20–0.40 SD (working paper).
- The Bulte et al. published title says "in the Workplace"; the NBER title says "in Uganda".
- Gallagher & Updegraff: online-first year 2011; cite the 2012 print year.

## Not citable from the dossier
- The original prospect-theory and loss-aversion papers are not in the dossier; the skill describes the mechanism without a citation.
- No LMIC SMS-framing trial was found; the Malawi worked example is illustrative, not a result.

## What to check locally
1. Does this population say the bad outcome aloud? Loss language about a child or a pregnancy may be unacceptable regardless of any effect.
2. Is the behavior prevention or detection as the population experiences it, not only as the program's logic has it?
3. Does the channel let the team randomize the frame, so the choice can be tested rather than assumed?
4. If money is involved, is anything in the loss variant already considered the person's own (wages, stipends, benefits)?
5. After the test: did the loss variant cause complaints, drop-outs, or distress, whatever the behavior result?
