# Matching a team's meaning to theory (steps 4 to 7)

Use this to turn what a team says about agency into named constructs, and to place them on the 2x2 agency framework. The frameworks are lenses for the team's own meaning, not a menu to pick from; borrow from more than one where that fits better. Full citations and verification status are in `evidence-notes.md`.

## The 2x2 agency framework

Agency is a person's capability to navigate toward the future they want (Wu, Goldberg & Xu, 2026). It has no single fixed definition. Two distinctions from the blog give it structure:

- **Forms of agency:** agency **with respect to the end goal** (can users make progress on outcomes they value, such as health, learning, or livelihoods, through, around, or beyond the tool?) and agency **with respect to the digital tool** (can users understand, question, adapt, and control the tool, and decide when to rely on it?).
- **Manifestations of agency:** **internally experienced** agency (thoughts and feelings) and **externally expressed** agency (choices and actions).

| | With respect to the end goal | With respect to the digital tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Internally experienced** (thoughts and feelings) | Self-efficacy, perceived control, hope, goals of one's own | Felt control over the tool, confidence in judging its output, trust that tracks its reliability |
| **Externally expressed** (choices and actions) | Setting, revising, and acting on goals; decisions; persistence; outcomes | Questioning, adapting, or rejecting advice; choosing when to use it; acting without it |

Donald et al. (2020) draw the same internal and external line for women's agency, and both their evidence and pro-WEAI's (Malapit et al., 2019) find the two correlate only weakly, so one does not stand in for the other. The framework helps a team see what its definition covers; it is not a list every plan must fill.

When a team's meaning involves decisions made with a spouse, parents, or community, joint decision-making is not automatically less agency. Ask whether the person wants to be involved and could be if they wished (Alkire, 2008), and name the construct in those terms.

## The frameworks

### Bandura: agency as four processes (psychology)
Social cognitive theory treats agency as intentionality (making and committing to plans), forethought (setting goals and anticipating consequences), self-reactiveness (monitoring progress and adjusting course), and self-reflectiveness (reflecting on experience to update beliefs and goals) (Bandura, 2001). It names three modes: direct personal agency, proxy agency (getting others with expertise or power to act on one's behalf), and collective agency. Self-efficacy, the belief in one's capability to act, is its best-known construct.
- **Fits when** the processes are observable, as in conversation logs, plans, or revisions, and when the team's words are about confidence or capability.
- **For AI tools,** proxy agency is the key idea: relying on a tool can be agency exercised through a proxy, not only agency lost.

### Sen and the capability approach (economics and development)
Agency is "what a person can do in line with his or her conception of the good" (Sen, 1985, as quoted by Alkire, 2008). The approach separates agency from well-being (a tool can raise one and lower the other), control (making the choice) from effective power (outcomes going the way one would have chosen), and autonomy (pursuing one's own goals) from ability (pursuing goals one has reason to value). Alkire (2008) notes that common measures capture control rather than effective freedom.
- **Fits when** norms, family, institutions, or money decide whether agency can be used, and when use of a tool may not turn into anything meaningful in users' lives (Prunkl, 2024, discusses this gap for AI).

### Kabeer: resources, agency, achievements (gender and development)
Empowerment is the process by which people denied the ability to make strategic life choices acquire it, through resources, agency, and achievements that are "indivisible in determining the meaning of an indicator" (Kabeer, 1999). Tool access is a resource; agency is what users do with it; achievements are the outcomes.
- **Fits when** a team says "empowerment" and needs to say which part it means.

### Donald et al.: goal-setting, sense of agency, acting on goals (women's agency)
Three interacting dimensions (Donald et al., 2020): goal-setting (goals that stem from one's own values), sense of agency (perceived control and ability), and acting on goals (decision-making, negotiation, bargaining, community participation). The World Bank's MAGNET work builds on this (Donald & Hernandez-de-Benito, 2021).
- **Fits when** the users face structural constraints, especially women and girls in low- and middle-income settings.

### Self-determination theory: autonomy as a need
Autonomy, competence, and relatedness are basic psychological needs (Ryan & Deci, 2000). The Relative Autonomy Index asks why people act (to avoid trouble, to please others, or because they value it), which describes the quality of agency rather than its presence (Ryan & Connell, 1989; used in Chad by Vaz, Pratley & Alkire, 2016).
- **Fits when** the team needs to tell endorsed reliance on a tool from compliance or drift.
- **Caution:** individualistic wording can change meaning across cultures (Chen et al., 2015).

### Jigsaw: five dimensions AI can strengthen or weaken (human-AI interaction)
From ethnographic research with early adopters of AI chatbots (Beacock et al., 2026, the probable underlying study): instrumental (getting tasks done), cognitive (thinking clearly and forming one's own views), affective (understanding and managing emotions), relational (building and navigating connections; the blog calls it social), and structural (navigating and shaping systems). The authors caution that immediate boosts to perceived agency may not translate into material effects or long-term capacity.
- **Fits when** naming which kind of agency a specific AI feature helps or erodes.
- **Caution:** 51 daily chatbot users in the United States, Germany, and Singapore; a preprint.

### HCI and reliance: agency with respect to the tool
A review of 161 HCI papers (Bennett et al., 2023) finds "agency" and "autonomy" used as vague umbrella terms and asks studies to specify self-causality and identity, material versus experiential agency, time-scale, and independence versus interdependence. Automation and human control are separate axes: a tool can do a lot while users stay in control (Shneiderman, 2020). The target is calibrated reliance, trust and use that track the tool's actual reliability (Lee & See, 2004), avoiding both over-reliance and ignoring good advice (Parasuraman & Riley, 1997). Deferring to an AI is not by itself disempowerment (Sharma et al., 2026).

### The Agency Fund's program lens
Programs that nurture adaptive goal formation, equip people to navigate complex decision environments, and support resilience after setbacks (Walsh & Madon, 2024). These map onto goal-setting, decision-making, and persistence.

## What erosion looks like, by cell

| | With respect to the end goal | With respect to the digital tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Internally experienced** | Confidence rises while capability does not; goals narrow to what the tool suggests | Confidence in the tool outgrows confidence in oneself (Lee et al., 2025); feeling unable to question it |
| **Externally expressed** | Assisted performance up, unassisted performance down (Bastani et al., 2025); goals abandoned when the tool is unavailable | Answer-seeking instead of working the problem; accepting wrong advice; handing over decisions the user would rather keep |

Where gains in agency could provoke a reaction at home or in the community, note it; gains in women's agency can provoke backlash (Bulte & Lensink, 2019).

## When nothing fits

There is no single agreed definition or scale: a review of 34 studies found almost every one used a different instrument, mostly generic rather than context-, culture-, or gender-specific (Cavazzoni, Fiorini & Veronese, 2022). If the team's meaning has no good match, keep their wording as a local construct and, where search tools are available, look for a framework built for this population: "Theories and frameworks of [the team's meaning] for [population] in [domain]. For each: what it foregrounds, what it can and cannot detect, and its main critiques. Cite sources."
