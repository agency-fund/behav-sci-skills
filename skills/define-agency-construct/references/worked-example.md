# Worked example: an AI mentor for student entrepreneurs

An illustrative run, built on the hypothetical example in Wu, Goldberg & Xu (2026). The program, the quotes, and the decisions are invented; nothing here is a finding.

**Input, gathered one question at a time:** "Pitch Partner" is a WhatsApp AI mentor used by Class 11 students in government schools in Delhi during a 12-week entrepreneurship course, in a mix of Hindi and English, mostly on a parent's phone in the evening. The course ends with a pitch scored by a panel. Asked what they mean by agency, the team said: "Students come up with a business idea that is really theirs, feel confident they can make it work, keep improving it when the mentor or the panel pushes back, and can tell when the mentor's suggestion is a bad one." Their worry is that the mentor's default "direct" mode, which suggests ideas straight away, makes students lean on it. They track messages per student and thumbs-up on replies, and mentioned both as early signs of success.

# Agency definition: Pitch Partner (AI mentor for student entrepreneurs)

**Context:** Class 11 students in Delhi government schools use a WhatsApp AI mentor during a 12-week course to develop a business idea they will pitch to a panel; the mentor is meant to help them think, not think for them.
**In your words:** "Students come up with a business idea that is really theirs, feel confident they can make it work, keep improving it when the mentor or the panel pushes back, and can tell when the mentor's suggestion is a bad one."
**Working definition:** A student's capability to form, defend, and revise a business idea of their own, and to judge when the mentor's input helps and when it does not.
**Not agency:** messages per student measure engagement; thumbs-up measure satisfaction. Students can chat a lot and like the replies while the mentor does the thinking.

## Your definition, matched to theory
| # | What you said | Construct | Theory |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "a business idea that is really theirs" | Ownership of the goal: the idea comes from the student's own aims and values, not the mentor's | Goal-setting (Donald et al.); relative autonomy (self-determination theory) |
| 2 | "feel confident they can make it work" | Entrepreneurial self-efficacy: belief in one's capability to develop and pitch a viable idea | Bandura, social cognitive theory |
| 3 | "keep improving it when the mentor or the panel pushes back" | Revising after feedback | Bandura: self-reactiveness |
| 4 | "can tell when the mentor's suggestion is a bad one" | Calibrated reliance: following the mentor when it is right and not when it is wrong | Lee & See, trust in automation; Jigsaw's cognitive dimension |

## Where it sits in the 2x2 agency framework
| | With respect to the end goal: a viable business idea | With respect to the digital tool: the AI mentor |
|---|---|---|
| **Internally experienced** (thoughts and feelings) | Entrepreneurial self-efficacy (2); ownership of the goal (1) | Open: e.g., feeling free to question or reject the mentor's advice |
| **Externally expressed** (choices and actions) | Revising after feedback (3) | Calibrated reliance (4) |

**Focus:** both expressed cells, plus self-efficacy. "The worry is about what students do with the mentor's suggestions, so we care most about revising and about pushing back; confidence matters because the panel rewards it."
**Not covered by this focus:** whether students feel able to question the mentor; a student who silently disagrees but goes along with it would look like calibrated reliance in the logs.
**Erosion would look like:** asking the mentor to choose the idea; pasting the mentor's wording into the pitch; no revision after critique; being unable to explain the idea in class without the phone. Asking the mentor to check the arithmetic in a budget, by choice, is a proxy, not erosion.
