# Evidence notes: design-commitment-device

Annotated bibliography for the skill. Every entry is copied from the library's evidence dossier (`docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md`, entry `design-commitment-device`, plus sections A and B) with the dossier's verification tag. Nothing was added from memory; a number appears only as the dossier writes it. The dossier records no unverified items for this entry.

## Anchor sources

**Bryan, G., Karlan, D., & Nelson, S. (2010).** Commitment devices. *Annual Review of Economics*, 2, 671–698. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.economics.102308.124324 [verified-crossref]
- Narrative review, not a meta-analysis. Defines a commitment device as a voluntary arrangement that restricts future choice or attaches a penalty to it, taken up by people who anticipate their own present bias. The dossier records no pooled effect size from it, and none exists among this skill's sources.

**Ashraf, N., Karlan, D., & Yin, W. (2006).** Tying Odysseus to the mast: Evidence from a commitment savings product in the Philippines. *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 121(2), 635–672. https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.2006.121.2.635 [verified-crossref]
- SEED, Green Bank, Butuan, Philippines. A savings account locked until a date or amount the client chose. 1,777 clients surveyed, 710 offered the product; 28.4% took it up; savings balances rose 81 percentage points versus control at 12 months. The take-up figure is the source of the skill's "most people decline" rule.

**Giné, X., Karlan, D., & Zinman, J. (2010).** Put your money where your butt is: A commitment contract for smoking cessation. *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics*, 2(4), 213–235. https://doi.org/10.1257/app.2.4.213 [verified-crossref]
- CARES, Mindanao, Philippines. A deposit contract forfeited on a failed urine test. 11% take-up; those offered were 3.3 to 5.8 percentage points more likely to pass the 6-month test, persisting at 12 months; 66% of committers still failed and lost their money. The source of the skill's failure plan: in a hard device a majority of committers may lose their stake.

**Dolan, P., Hallsworth, M., Halpern, D., King, D., Metcalfe, R., & Vlaev, I. (2012).** Influencing behaviour: The mindspace way. *Journal of Economic Psychology*, 33(1), 264–277. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joep.2011.10.009 [verified-crossref]
- MINDSPACE, nine levers including "Commitments". The practitioner statement of the technique; a framework, not an effect.

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)

- **DellaVigna, S., & Linos, E. (2022).** RCTs to scale. *Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709 [verified-crossref]. 126 RCTs, 23 million people. Academic journal studies average 8.7 percentage points (+33.4%); nudge-unit trials average 1.4 points (+8.0%). About 70% of the gap is selective publication plus low power.
- **Ferraro, P. J., & Tracy, J. D. (2022).** *Experimental Economics*, 25(5), 1441–1466. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-022-09754-x [verified-crossref]. Loss-framed incentives: pooled 0.16 SD; 0.33 SD in the lab versus 0.02 SD in the field, with publication bias among lab studies. Relevant here because a forfeit is a loss frame; the field estimate is the one to plan on.
- **Milkman, K. L., et al. (2021).** Megastudies improve the impact of applied behavioural science. *Nature*, 600, 478–483. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04128-4 [verified-crossref]. 61,293 people, 54 interventions in a US gym; 45% raised visits by 9 to 27%; only 8% of interventions produced effects that persisted after the program; expert forecasts failed. Cited for the persistence warning: plan to measure after the lock ends.
- Pattern the dossier asks every technique skill to state: published lab or academic effects should be discounted when transferred. For this skill the canonical numbers are already field, intent-to-treat effects of a few points; the discount applies mainly to take-up, which the dossier puts at 10 to 30% in general and which was 11% and 28.4% in the two trials.

## Non-WEIRD evidence

- Both canonical trials are Philippine (Butuan and Mindanao), so the evidence base is non-WEIRD. The dossier notes several further LMIC successes without naming them; none is cited here.

## Do not cite

- No meta-analysis of commitment devices: the dossier found none, and the skill says so rather than borrowing a number from the nudge meta-analyses.
- Any claim about where CARES forfeits went or about the SEED interest rate: not recorded in the dossier, so the skill states only the design rule (route forfeits away from the program) as its own judgment.

## What to check locally

- Whether the barrier is present bias rather than cost: do people have the money at some point and lose it to spending or requests, or never have it?
- Whether the institution that would hold the stake is trusted to return it, and whether a balance it holds is visible to kin who would ask for it.
- The income calendar: when a lock or a forfeit would coincide with a lean season, school fees, or debt repayment.
- Whether a forfeit reads locally as a fine from the program, which changes the relationship with the holder.
- What informal commitment people already use (a treasurer, a savings group, a neighbor who holds cash), which tells the team the restriction people accept and the take-up to expect.
