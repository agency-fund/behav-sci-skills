# Worked example: school-fee savings among peri-urban workers in Uganda

Illustrative run on an invented program. Names, amounts, and shares are made up to show the skill's shape; none is a finding.

**Setting.** "Fees Ready" (illustrative), a savings program for casual and piece-rate workers in Wakiso district, run with a mobile-money operator that can open a locked sub-wallet with a worker-chosen unlock date and a worker-chosen amount. The barrier came from `decompose-comb-barriers` fieldwork; the lever brief from `select-intervention-levers` selected a commitment product.

**Inputs supplied.** Target behavior: workers deposit UGX 10,000 a week into a school-fees wallet from the first week of each term until the next term's fees are due. Interview round (60 workers, illustrative): 52 say they intend to save for fees, 44 say they have UGX 10,000 spare most weeks, and 41 say it is gone within two days on food top-ups, airtime, and requests from relatives. Income is weekly and drops in the rainy months of April and May and October and November when construction slows. About a quarter already give cash to a trusted shopkeeper to hold. The operator can show the locked balance on request and is trusted for wage payments.

```markdown
# Commitment device specification

**Target behavior:** Casual and piece-rate workers enrolled in Fees Ready in Wakiso district deposit UGX 10,000 into a locked school-fees wallet every Friday from the first week of term until the next term's fees are due.
**Barrier confirmation:** Intention stated by 52 of 60 workers interviewed; 44 have the amount spare most weeks; 41 say it is gone within two days on top-ups, airtime, and relatives' requests (illustrative). Cost and access are ruled out for most; the barrier is spending and requests at the moment the cash is in hand.
**Device type:** Hard, as a lock without forfeit. The balance is verifiable, the holder is trusted, and a lock protects against relatives' requests ("it is locked, I cannot send it") without taking money from a worker who misses a week.

## Goal and verification
- Goal: a worker-chosen unlock date set to the week fees are due, and a worker-chosen target amount; the default shown is UGX 10,000 a week to the fee date.
- Verified by: the operator's locked-wallet balance, visible to the worker on request at no cost; no third-party test is needed.

## Stake
- What the person puts up: access to their own deposits until the unlock date.
- Range the person chooses within: weekly deposit UGX 2,000 to 20,000; an early-unlock request is allowed once per term with a two-day delay, so the lock bites against the evening request but not against a genuine emergency. The ceiling is set at 20,000 because the rainy-month income dip (illustrative) would make a larger weekly lock a hardship.
- Where forfeited stakes go: nothing is forfeited in this design. If a forfeit version is piloted later, forfeits go to a school-fee fund for the same community, never to the program.

## Opt-in rule
- How the offer is made: at the wage-payment point, by the operator's agent, with a one-page explanation in Luganda and English.
- The explicit "no" option: the agent records "declined" as an outcome; no follow-up offer for a term.
- Not bundled with: airtime bundles, wage advances, or any loan product.

## Failure plan
- What a failed committer loses: nothing in money; a worker who stops depositing keeps the balance locked to the date, then receives it.
- How they learn and whether they may try again: a monthly balance SMS; a worker who missed four weeks is offered a smaller weekly amount for the next term, not dropped.
- How failures are counted in the evaluation: "stopped depositing before the unlock date" and "used the early-unlock option" are pre-specified outcomes alongside the balance at the fee date.

## Expected take-up and effect
- Take-up: planned at 15% of those offered (illustrative), near the low end of the 10 to 30% range, with the shopkeeper practice (a quarter already use an informal holder) as the reason it could be higher.
- Effect among those offered: a few percentage points more workers paying fees on time at term start, measured on all workers offered, not only those who enrolled.

## Fairness
- Who takes up: workers with steadier weekly surplus and fewer dependents' requests.
- Who loses: in this no-forfeit design, no one loses money; workers who lock and then face an emergency bear a two-day delay.
- Could the stake cause hardship: no, with the UGX 20,000 ceiling and the early-unlock route; a forfeit version would change this answer and is not specified here.
- Open items for audit-intervention-ethics: whether the two-day delay is acceptable in a medical emergency; whether the locked balance is visible to anyone but the worker.

## What to check before relying on this
- That the barrier is spending and requests, not an income shortfall in the rainy months; the interview round covered one dry-season month only (illustrative).
```

**What happened next (illustrative).** The specification went to `draft-lever-content` for the Luganda offer script and to `plan-evaluation-design` with take-up, early unlock, and stopped deposits as pre-specified outcomes. No results are reported here.
