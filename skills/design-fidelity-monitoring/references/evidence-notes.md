## The fidelity framework

- The anchor citation. The dossier records the citation and the instruction "identify essential components; define observable indicators; set sampling frequency; set a feedback loop to implementers".

## Practice source

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)
The skill does not quote the section A numbers, because it designs monitoring rather than an intervention. The pattern is still relevant to how the plan is used: DellaVigna & Linos (2022) [verified-crossref] report that academic-journal nudges average 8.7 percentage points against 1.4 points in nudge-unit trials, with about 70% of the gap explained by selective publication and low power; a fidelity plan is what lets a team tell whether its smaller field effect reflects the intervention or its delivery.

## Judgment calls that are the authors' own, not published criteria
- Adherence, dose, and quality get separate indicators per component.
- Every essential component has at least one data source independent of the implementer's own log.
- Every threshold names an action and a role.
- The feedback loop is task-level and is not used for appraisal, pay, or discipline, on the reasoning that a log that can punish the person who fills it in will be managed rather than filled in. No study of this is recorded in the dossier.

## What to check locally
1. Are registers and logs filled in during the visit or reconstructed later, and by whom?
2. Is unannounced observation acceptable to implementers and to the community, and what has "monitoring" meant here before?
3. Does each implementer have a supervisor who can act on a threshold within the month?
4. Can a random sample of participants be re-contacted for a back-check (phone ownership, consent, cost)?
5. Will the implementing organization put the non-punitive data-use rule in writing?
6. Which component the team is least sure is essential, and whether the pilot can settle it.
