## Mechanism

### Wood & Neal (2007) [verified-crossref]
Wood, W., & Neal, D. T. (2007). A new look at habits and the habit–goal interface. *Psychological Review*, 114(4). https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.114.4.843
- Theory paper. Habits as context-cue to response links formed by repetition; once formed they run with little goal deliberation, which is why they can outlast the goal or prompt that started them.
- The report had this from memory; the DOI was located in the audit.

## Timeline

### Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010) [verified-crossref]
Lally, P., van Jaarsveld, C. H. M., Potts, H. W. W., & Wardle, J. (2010). How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world. *European Journal of Social Psychology*, 40, 998–1009. https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674. Online-first 2009.
- 96 volunteers; each chose one new daily behavior tied to a daily cue; automaticity self-reported daily; some missing data.
- Median 66 days to the automaticity plateau; range 18–254 days.
- Observational modeling of self-reports, not a randomized trial. The dossier records no country breakdown; the skill treats it as a WEIRD research setting.

### Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012) [verified-crossref]
Gardner, B., Lally, P., & Wardle, J. (2012). Making health habitual: The psychology of 'habit-formation' and general practice. *British Journal of General Practice*, 62(605), 664–666. https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp12X659466
- Practice piece translating the above for general practitioners. The dossier draws from these two sources the boundary conditions used here: a stable existing cue ("after breakfast"), a behavior shrunk to its minimum, a tick sheet, and roughly two to three months rather than 21 days.

## Persistence

### Milkman et al. (2021) [verified-crossref]
Milkman, K. L., et al. (2021). Megastudies improve the impact of applied behavioural science. *Nature*, 600, 478–483. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04128-4
- 61,293 people, 54 interventions, US gym. 45% of interventions raised visits by 9–27%; only 8% produced effects that persisted after the program; expert forecasts failed.

### Blumenstock, Callen & Ghani (2018) [verified-crossref]
Blumenstock, J., Callen, M., & Ghani, T. (2018). Why do defaults affect behavior? Experimental evidence from Afghanistan. *American Economic Review*, 108(10), 2868–2901. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20171676
- 949 employees of one firm (Roshan, mobile money) in Afghanistan. A 5% default contribution versus 0% raised participation by 40 points, equivalent to a 50% employer match; the mechanism was present bias and the cognitive cost of deciding, not endorsement or inattention; savings attitudes and habits persisted after the trial.
- Relevance here: persistence of a repeated behavior in an LMIC after the intervention ended. It is not a test of cue-anchoring.

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)

### DellaVigna & Linos (2022) [verified-crossref]
*Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709
- 126 RCTs, 23 million people. Academic-journal nudges average 8.7 percentage points; nudge-unit trials 1.4 points. About 70% of the gap is selective publication combined with low power.

### Pattern the dossier asks every technique skill to state
Lab-to-field shrinkage in loss framing (0.33 to 0.02 SD; Ferraro & Tracy, 2022, dossier A9) and the academic-versus-unit gap (8.7 vs 1.4 points) point the same way: discount published lab or academic effects when transferring them to a field program.

## Why each cue-stability criterion matters
- **Every day:** repetition is the mechanism; a cue that skips days skips repetitions.
- **Same place:** the context is part of the cue (Wood & Neal); a cue that moves is a different cue each time.
- **Already automatic:** a cue the person has to remember is itself a behavior needing a cue.
- **Immediately before the behavior can be done:** a gap between cue and action lets other things intervene.
- **Needs present:** a link that fires when the behavior cannot be done teaches a link to not doing it.
- **Not program-controlled:** a cue that ends with the program ends the habit.
These are the skill's operationalization of "stable context"; the dossier does not test the criteria separately.

## Not citable from the dossier

- Fogg, *Tiny Habits* (2019), is listed in the dossier as unverified and is not cited.
- The dossier records nothing on whether a missed day resets habit formation; the skill's "a miss is a miss, not a reset" is a design instruction, not a finding.
- No LMIC trial of cue-anchoring was found; the Kenya worked example is illustrative.

## What to check locally
1. Which daily cues are genuinely fixed across seasons, religious calendars, and household roles?
2. Is what the behavior needs present where the cue happens?
3. Is a daily tick sheet acceptable, private enough, and usable by people with low literacy?
4. Can the program actually fade its reminders, or does the funder expect them to continue?
5. Did the behavior persist one to three months after support stopped, measured rather than assumed?
