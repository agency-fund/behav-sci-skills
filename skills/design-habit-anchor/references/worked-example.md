# Worked example: daily M-Pesa savings deposit by market vendors, Kenya

A savings program works with female stall vendors in Nairobi's Gikomba and Toi markets. `select-intervention-levers` chose habit formation for a confirmed barrier: vendors intend to save, but the deposit does not happen on most days because nothing in the end-of-day routine prompts it, and the daily SMS reminder the program sends is ignored after the first weeks. Vendor interviews supplied the candidate cues. All numbers are illustrative; this is not a real result.

Inputs the user gave: the behavior (vendors deposit a fixed amount into the M-Pesa savings wallet each trading day before leaving the market; "that day's net surplus" was dropped because vendors could not compute it reliably at closing); frequency (six trading days a week); candidate cues from vendor interviews (counting the float at closing, packing stock into the lock-up, paying the market fee collector mid-morning, the matatu ride home, morning tea before opening); what the behavior needs (the phone with M-Pesa; the savings-lock menu set up at enrollment); reminders (a daily SMS at 5 pm, running six weeks, which the program can stop); not a clinical behavior.

```markdown
# Habit anchor plan

**Behavior:** Female stall vendors in Gikomba and Toi markets deposit a fixed amount into their M-Pesa savings wallet each trading day before leaving the market.
**Minimum repeatable version:** One deposit of KSh 50 (illustrative), the same every day regardless of takings; small enough to do on a slow day, so a bad day's trade does not break the run of repetitions.
**Frequency:** Each trading day, six days a week; near-daily, so the daily-behavior evidence applies with a small stretch.

## Cue
**Chosen cue:** Counting the float at closing, before packing stock into the lock-up.
| Check | Answer |
|---|---|
| Happens every day | Yes, every trading day; vendors say it happens even on a day with almost no sales. |
| Same place each time | Yes, at the stall. |
| Already automatic for the person | Yes; vendors describe it as the first thing they do at closing, without thinking. |
| Immediately before the behavior can be done | Yes; the phone is in hand or at the stall, and the deposit takes under a minute. |
| What the behavior needs is present at that moment | Phone and M-Pesa menu, yes; the savings-lock menu must be set up at enrollment. |
| Not controlled by the program | Yes. |
**Rejected cues:** Paying the fee collector (time varies; the collector sometimes skips a day); the matatu ride (not every vendor rides; crowded, phone out of reach); morning tea (before trading, so the deposit would come from yesterday's cash, which has usually gone home already).

## Link statement
"After I count the float at closing, I will send KSh 50 to my savings wallet at the stall."

## Tick sheet
A card the size of the float pouch, one box per trading day for 12 weeks, kept in the pouch; one pen mark per deposit. For the vendor's own eyes; the program reads deposits from M-Pesa records, not from the card. Boxes, not words, so it works regardless of literacy.

## Repetition period
Twelve weeks (about three months), because the one timed study found a median of 66 days to automaticity with a range of 18 to 254 in 96 volunteers who rated their own automaticity; near-daily rather than daily behavior and a field setting argue for the longer end. Vendors are told: a missed day is a missed day; start again at the next closing.

## Supports to fade
The 5 pm SMS reminder stops at the end of week 12. From week 9 it drops to three days a week, so the count at closing, not the phone buzzing, is what carries the deposit.

## Persistence check
- **At the end of the period:** Share of trading days with a deposit in weeks 10 to 12, from M-Pesa records.
- **After support stops:** The same share in weeks 17 to 20 (one to two months after the last SMS), plus one item at the week-20 visit: "I send the KSh 50 without having to think about it" (agree/disagree), labeled as self-report.

## What to check before relying on this
- Whether counting the float at closing is as fixed as vendors say during the December peak and the April rains, when closing routines reportedly change.
- The timeline rests on 96 volunteers in a WEIRD research setting using self-report; expect a share of vendors never to reach automaticity, and treat the week-17-to-20 figure, not the week-12 figure, as the result.
```

What the skill did not do: write the card or the SMS text (`draft-lever-content`), design the fading SMS schedule in detail (`design-reminder-schedule`), or set up the evaluation of the persistence check (`plan-evaluation-design`).
