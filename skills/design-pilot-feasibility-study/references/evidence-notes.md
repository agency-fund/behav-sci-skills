## Anchor sources

**Eldridge, S. M., et al. (2016). CONSORT 2010 statement: Extension to randomised pilot and feasibility trials. *BMJ*, 355, i5239. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i5239** [verified-crossref]
- The reporting standard for randomized pilot and feasibility trials. The dossier records no numbers and no item list from it; SKILL.md uses it for the principle that a pilot's objectives are feasibility objectives, reported against progression criteria.
- The report skeleton in SKILL.md is "in the spirit of" the extension; it is not a reproduction of the checklist, which the dossier did not extract.

**Bowen, D. J., et al. (2009). How we design feasibility studies. *American Journal of Preventive Medicine*, 36(5), 452–457. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amepre.2009.02.002** [verified-crossref]
- The design guide that separates "can it be done and accepted" from "does it work". The dossier records no numbers and does not list Bowen's areas of focus; SKILL.md's five domains (recruitment, retention, fidelity, acceptability, data completeness) are the dossier's four (recruitment, retention, fidelity, acceptability) plus data completeness, added by this skill.

**EGAP. *10 things to know about pilot studies*. https://egap.org/resource/10-things-to-know-about-pilot-studies/** [verified-fetch, listing only]
- Confirmed as a listing on EGAP's methods-guides page; the guide itself was not opened by the dossier's audit. Cite as a practitioner guide, not for specific claims.

## Dossier notes used directly

- Replication status (dossier): "method guidance; pilots are often wrongly used to estimate effect sizes for power." This is the documented misuse SKILL.md tells the model to refuse.
- Boundary conditions (dossier): "define feasibility questions (recruitment, retention, fidelity, acceptability); set go/no-go progression thresholds; size for estimation, not significance." SKILL.md turns go/no-go into go / amend / stop; the three-way split is this skill's convention.
- J-PAL questionnaire piloting resource: listed in the dossier as a practitioner source from the report, not fetched; no URL recorded. Not cited in SKILL.md.

## Known corrections and cautions

- No corrections listed in the dossier for this entry.
- No threshold values, pilot sizes, or precision rules appear in the dossier. Every number in the worked example is illustrative, and SKILL.md gives no default threshold; thresholds are derived from the main design the pilot serves.
- SKILL.md's statement that the number needed to estimate a rate to a usable margin is usually far below the number needed to detect an effect is a qualitative statistical point, not a quoted figure; the margin itself is to be confirmed with a statistician.

## What to check locally

1. How recruitment actually happens here: gatekeepers, community entry, consent norms, and who has to agree before anyone is approached.
2. Whether the data system the main trial assumes (registers, pill counts, transaction logs) exists at the pilot sites in the same form.
3. Whether the pilot season matches the main trial's season (planting, school terms, rains, migration), since recruitment and retention rates move with timing.
4. Whether the pilot sites are typical of the main trial's sites, or the most cooperative ones.
5. What local ethics review requires for a pilot that touches medication, children, or vulnerable participants, including a stopping rule for harm.
