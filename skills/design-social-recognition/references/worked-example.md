# Worked example: timely antenatal visits at four health centers in Dedza district, Malawi

An illustrative run. A district health program in Dedza, Malawi, with four health centers and their Health Surveillance Assistants (HSAs, the community health cadre) wants to raise timely antenatal care (ANC) attendance. All counts, prices, and facts below are illustrative, not findings.

**Input (condensed):** Behavior: pregnant women registered at the four program health centers attend their second, third, and fourth antenatal visits by the week the health passport schedules each. Verification: the ANC register and the health passport stamp, both completed by the ANC nurse at the visit. Reference group (from `map-reference-network`): the village mother group that meets fortnightly with the HSA, and the woman's mother-in-law; both regard keeping ANC appointments as "caring for the pregnancy" (illustrative finding). `diagnose-social-norm` found the behavior valued but invisible. Stigma: pregnancy itself is not stigmatized once visible; early disclosure is sensitive for adolescent and unmarried women. Budget: about MK 400 per woman. Baseline timely ANC4: 31% (illustrative). About 30% of the catchment lives more than 8 km from a center, and the HSAs say those women come late because of distance. Four other centers in the district can serve as a comparison.

# Social recognition design

**Target behavior:** Pregnant women registered at the four program health centers attend their second, third, and fourth antenatal visits by the week the health passport schedules each.
**Completion is verified by:** The ANC nurse, who stamps the health passport and enters the visit date in the ANC register; the band and each bead are handed over only after the register entry is made.
**Reference group (from map-reference-network):** Fellow members of the village mother group and the woman's mother-in-law; both regard timely ANC as caring for the pregnancy (illustrative).

## Stigma and privacy screen (gate)
Would women want it known that they attended on time? Yes, among the mother group and the family, once the pregnancy is visible. Would they want it known that they did not? Mostly a matter of circumstance (distance), addressed below. Early disclosure is sensitive for adolescent and unmarried women, so nothing is given at the first visit, and any woman may decline the band with no note in the register. Verdict: proceed, with the first-visit exclusion and the opt-out.

## The signal
"A woman wearing the green band is read by the mother group and her in-laws as a woman who takes care of her pregnancy." The group meets fortnightly with the HSA and will see the bands there; mothers-in-law see them at home.

## Token
- **Form:** Wearable. A woven green wrist band in the district health color, with a small wooden bead added at each later timely visit (up to two beads).
- **Why this form:** Seen at the mother group and at home; green is the color women in the district already associate with the health centers (illustrative; to be checked against party and church colors before launch); lasts the pregnancy; about MK 350 per band and bead set, within budget; nothing like it is sold at the market.
- **Hard to get without completing:** Bands are stamped with the center code, issued only to ANC nurses, and counted against the register monthly by the program officer.

## Completion rule
- **Earns the token:** The band at a timely second visit; a bead at each timely third and fourth visit. Timely means by the scheduled week in the passport.
- **Late completers:** No bead for a late visit, but the band itself is earned at any later timely visit, so the path stays open through the pregnancy.
- **Stages, if any:** Band, bead, bead.

## Non-recipients
- **Who will visibly lack it, and why:** Women who book after 20 weeks (common) and women more than 8 km from a center, about 30% of the catchment, who come late because of distance rather than choice.
- **What lacking it will be read as:** At worst, as not caring for the pregnancy, which would be unfair for the distant 30%.
- **Mitigations:** Outreach ANC days at two distant villages each month count as visits and carry the band, agreed before launch; the HSAs tell mother groups that the band marks visits that fell on time, not good motherhood, and never name women without one; no mark on the child or the household; a woman may decline the band.

## Delivery
- **Handed over by, when:** The ANC nurse, at the visit, after the register entry.
- **What they say:** One line linking the band to the next scheduled visit date; final Chichewa wording from draft-lever-content and the pre-test.
- **Stock and replacement:** 300 bands and 600 beads per center per quarter (illustrative); replacements for a lost band only against the register.

## Measurement plan
- **Uptake:** Share of registered women with the second, third, and fourth visits by the scheduled week, from the ANC registers, by center and by distance band.
- **Signal integrity:** HSAs note bands seen at mother groups and the program officer matches a sample against the register each quarter.
- **Harm:** Four questions in the mother-group follow-up each quarter: has anyone been teased or asked why she has no band; has anyone stayed away from the group because of it; has any woman been questioned at home. Any report is reviewed with the HSA supervisor.
- **Comparison and pre-registration:** The four comparison centers, same period; outcomes, the distance split, and the harm questions filed before launch.
- **Expected effect:** Below the Sierra Leone 14.4 points; the direction is supported by two trials, the size here is unknown; a null is possible.

## Evidence statement (mandatory)
Two trials in low-income settings support visible recognition: in Sierra Leone, bracelets for timely vaccination raised timely complete vaccination by 14.4 points, about 56% to 71%, in a study verified only as a registry record; in Zambia, public recognition improved hairdressers' condom sales, more for prosocially motivated agents, with an unverified effect size. Neither tested antenatal visits or Malawi. The design is cheap and reversible; its main risk is marking women who live far from care, which the outreach days and the opt-out are meant to limit, and which the harm questions are meant to detect.

## What to check before relying on this
- Whether green bands already signal a party, a church, or another program in these villages; one question to each mother group before ordering.
- Whether the distant 30% can in fact reach the outreach days; if not, the band marks distance and the design should pause.
