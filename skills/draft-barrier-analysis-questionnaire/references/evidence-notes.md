# Evidence notes: draft-barrier-analysis-questionnaire

Every item below is taken from `docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md` (section C entries for this skill and for `analyze-doer-nondoer-differences`, and section B item B16). The verification tags are the dossier's. Nothing was added from memory; where the dossier gives no number, none is quoted.

## Anchor sources

### Kittle, B. (2013; 2nd ed. 2017). *A Practical Guide to Conducting a Barrier Analysis*. Helen Keller International. [verified-fetch: https://coregroup.org/resource-library/a-practical-guide-to-conducting-a-barrier-analysis/]
- The instrument this skill reproduces. The fetched page shows Helen Keller International, "YEAR PUBLISHED 2013", and "Second Edition (2017)". The author name is not shown on the page; the attribution to Kittle comes from the labs report. Treat the author name as report-sourced.
- Determinants (twelve, per the labs report): self-efficacy, social norms, positive consequences, negative consequences, access, cues for action, perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, action efficacy, divine will, policy, culture.
- Sample rule: 45 Doers and 45 Non-Doers, derived from a case-control calculation (relative risk 3.0, 80% power). This is a design calculation, not an empirical finding about the tool.
- The companion analysis entry records: flag a determinant when the Doer/Non-Doer gap is at least 15 percentage points or p < .05 in the Barrier Analysis tabulation sheet (lessons 5 and 12); write "Bridges to Activities" for flagged determinants. That rule lives in `analyze-doer-nondoer-differences`, not here.

### Davis, T. (2010). *Barrier Analysis Facilitator's Guide*. Food for the Hungry. [verified-fetch: https://coregroup.org/resource-library/barrier-analysis-facilitators-guide/]
- The earlier guide. The fetched page shows Food for the Hungry and 2010, no author name (attribution again from the report), and describes the tool as "built around eight determinants".
- Correction recorded in the dossier: the 2010 guide says eight determinants while the 2013 Practical Guide uses twelve. The skill follows Kittle's twelve and says so in its output.

### CORE Group (2013). *Designing for Behavior Change: For Agriculture, Natural Resource Management, Health and Nutrition*. CORE Group, Food Security and Nutrition Network, USAID. [verified-fetch: https://coregroup.org/resource-library/designing-for-behavior-change-for-agriculture-natural-resource-management-health-and-nutrition/]
- The planning frame around Barrier Analysis. Built on AED's BEHAVE framework (per the fetched page, which does not mention TOPS).
- Key concepts the dossier records: Priority Group, Influencing Group, Determinants, "Bridges to Activities".

## Numbers quoted in the skill, and where they come from
- Twelve determinants: labs report, from Kittle.
- 45 Doers and 45 Non-Doers; relative risk 3.0; 80% power: Kittle 2013, per the dossier.
- Eight determinants in Davis 2010: the fetched coregroup.org page.
- No effect sizes exist for this method; none are quoted.

## Non-WEIRD evidence
- The method originated in LMIC water, sanitation, and nutrition programming and is "widely used" in many LMICs (labs report).
- Zambia: a facility-births Barrier Analysis by People in Need is cited in the labs report (no citation details in the dossier).
- CORE Group's "Powerful to Change" analysis links Barrier Analysis or Doer/Non-Doer use to the highest behavior change among the programs it reviewed. The dossier marks this as an association, not a validation; the skill repeats that caution.
- Save the Children use of Barrier Analysis: [unverified, author to confirm].
- Consequence for the skill: `weird: likely-generalizes`, because the method is LMIC-native, with the explicit caveat that generalizing is not the same as validated.

## Replication status (as recorded)
- Practitioner method; no psychometric validation of the questionnaire or the determinant list was found.
- The thresholds used downstream (15 points, p < .05) are conventions, not validated cutoffs.
- No publication-bias corrections apply, because there is no effect being estimated.

## Boundary conditions and cautions from the dossier
- Always include self-efficacy, social norms, and positive and negative consequences.
- Pilot the questionnaire.
- State which edition's determinant list the questionnaire follows.
- Author names for Kittle and Davis come from the report, not from the fetched pages.

## What the skill adds beyond the sources (judgment calls, not evidence)
- Screening questions written so they do not signal the Doer answer, with a preference for record or observation over self-report.
- Matched Doer and Non-Doer wording that differs only in tense and frame.
- Verbatim recording and post-fieldwork coding.
- A running order that starts with self-efficacy and ends with divine will, policy, and culture.
- Social norms asked as both who approves or disapproves (injunctive) and what most people like the respondent do (descriptive).

## What to check locally
- Whether Doer status can be verified for this behavior (card, register, observation) or rests on self-report, and how socially desirable the behavior is.
- Whether the recall window matches how often the behavior happens.
- Whether "divine will", "policy", and "culture" have a meaningful local reading for this behavior, and what words carry them.
- Who the enumerators are relative to the respondents (a health worker asking about her own service will shape answers).
- Whether 45 of each group can be recruited from the same communities without pooling unlike places.
- Whether the behavior needs ethics review before interviews begin.
