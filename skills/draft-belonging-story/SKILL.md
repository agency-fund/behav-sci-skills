## What it does

Drafts a set of stories from older peers that present early difficulty in a new setting as something most people go through before it passes.

A person unsure whether "people like me" belong reads each setback (a poor grade, a cold reply, a lonely week) as proof that they do not, pulls back, and so meets more setbacks. Stories from older peers of the same group who say "I felt that too, it was normal, it passed" change what a setback means; the recipient then writes advice to future peers, the "saying-is-believing" step that turns the message into their own position. The condition is affordance: the setting must in fact offer belonging. The large multi-site study raised first-year completion only where the group was afforded opportunities to belong; where it is not, the story is a false promise. All the evidence is from US colleges, and no trial in a low- or middle-income country was found. This skill writes the story set with those conditions; it does not decide whether belonging is the question.

## When to use it

### Use it when
- `identify-wise-intervention-target` named belonging ("do people like me belong here?") as the primary question and the affordance check passed
- Someone asks for "a belonging intervention", "testimonials from older students for orientation week", or "a way to tell new trainees the first weeks are hard for everyone"
- Early dropout at a transition is concentrated in one group (rural students, women in a male trade, first-generation entrants, a language minority) and the exits follow a first setback rather than a cost or a timetable
- A team is about to write the stories itself, or reuse a glossy success story, instead of collecting real accounts of early difficulty from peers of the group

### Do not use it when
- It is not yet known whether the question is belonging, ability, self-integrity, or trust; run `identify-wise-intervention-target`
- People read setbacks as proof of fixed ability ("I am not a math person"); that is `adapt-growth-mindset-message`
- The threat is to self-integrity, feeling judged in a stigmatized domain; that is `draft-values-affirmation`
- The group faces active hostility or exclusion in the setting (harassment, discrimination, staff who sort them out); the barrier is structural, a story would ask them to trust a setting that will not include them, and the route is `select-intervention-levers` plus the specialist section below
- The team needs other copy for a chosen technique (a welcome message, a reminder); that is `draft-lever-content`

## Before you start

Ask these before producing anything. Do not ask anything the user has already answered.

1. **Required.** The target behavior (persisting, completing, attending) as who, what, when; the transition moment; and the evidence, in people's own words, that early difficulty is read as "people like me do not belong here".
2. **Required.** Who the group is in its own terms (rural students, first-generation entrants, women in the trade, a language minority), and whether older peers of that same group who got through exist and can be interviewed. Without them the stories cannot be collected and the skill cannot run.
3. **Required.** The affordance facts: who in the setting will include them (peer mentors, staff from the group, a club, a hostel arrangement), and any evidence of hostility, harassment, or exclusion. Facts, not intentions.
4. **Required.** Delivery: when relative to the transition, the format (printed booklet, a session led by older peers, audio, a message series), the language and reading level, and who delivers.
5. Optional. The outcome data available (enrollment, attendance, completion records) and whether a test with a comparison group is planned.
6. Optional. Consent and anonymity rules for the storytellers, and whether the recipients' advice can be shared with the next intake.

If 1 or 2 is missing, ask and stop. If 3 is missing, ask once; if still unknown, produce the collection guide and the story criteria only, and mark the story set "not to be delivered until the affordance check is done". If 4 is missing, assume a session led by two older peers in the first week, with a printed story set, and flag it. If 5 is missing, state that persistence or completion must be the outcome. If 6 is missing, assume anonymized stories with written consent, and say so.

## What it draws on

- Walton, G. M., & Cohen, G. L. (2011). A brief social-belonging intervention improves academic and health outcomes of minority students. *Science*, 331(6023), 1447–1451. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198364
- Walton, G. M., et al. (2023). Where and with whom does a brief social-belonging intervention promote progress in college? *Science*, 380(6644), 499–505. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade4420 (Crossref lists an erratum, 10.1126/science.ads9718, 2024; author to check what it changes)
- Walton, G. M., & Wilson, T. D. (2018). Wise interventions: Psychological remedies for social and personal problems. *Psychological Review*, 125(5), 617–655. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000115
- DellaVigna, S., & Linos, E. (2022). RCTs to scale: Comprehensive evidence from two nudge units. *Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709
- Ferraro, P. J., & Tracy, J. D. (2022). A reassessment of the potential for loss-framed incentive contracts to increase productivity: A meta-analysis and a real-effort experiment. *Experimental Economics*, 25(5), 1441–1466. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-022-09754-x

Walton and Cohen's original study (N = 92, minority students at a US college) gave first-year students accounts from older students saying that worries about belonging are common in the first year and fade with time, then had the recipients write advice for future students; the achievement gap roughly halved over three years. Walton and Wilson place it among wise interventions: brief, aimed at one meaning (here, "a setback means people like me do not belong"), and made the person's own through the saying-is-believing step. The 2023 study (26,911 students at 22 institutions) found that the intervention raised full-time first-year completion only where the group was afforded opportunities to belong; the affordance gate in this skill is that finding. The judgment calls this skill adds: stories are collected from older peers of the same group, never written by the team; every story carries a concrete difficulty, the doubt it raised, and what changed; the saying-is-believing task is mandatory; delivery is once, before or at the transition; the outcome is persistence or completion; and the output states that no trial outside WEIRD settings was found, so a first use is a test.

How much to discount: the original is one small study (N = 92) with a large effect, and the large multi-site study found the effect only under a condition. That pattern, early large effects followed by smaller conditional ones, is the general one: academic journal trials average 8.7 percentage points where nudge-unit trials of the same kind average 1.4 (DellaVigna & Linos), and loss framing shrinks from 0.33 SD in the lab to 0.02 in the field (Ferraro & Tracy). Plan for a small change in completion among the target group where belonging is afforded, nothing elsewhere, and a null as a live outcome in a setting where the technique has never been tested.

**WEIRD skew:** All the evidence is from US colleges: the original with minority students, the 2023 study across 22 institutions. Check locally which group line matters (region, ethnicity, caste, language, gender, first generation), whether "belonging" has a local idiom or a different one is doing the work, whether written stories work or a session led by older peers is needed, and whether older peers of the group exist in the setting at all. **Non-WEIRD evidence:** none found in the sources this skill draws on; the dossier found no trial of a belonging story in a low- or middle-income country, so the first use there is a test, not a rollout.

**Replication status:** Replicated, with conditions. The N = 92 original was followed by the 26,911-student study across 22 institutions, which found the effect only where the group was afforded opportunities to belong. See `references/evidence-notes.md` for each source with its verification state.

## How to do it

1. **Carry over the target, the transition, and the quotes verbatim.** If the quotes are about ability or about feeling judged rather than about "people like me", stop and route to `adapt-growth-mindset-message` or `draft-values-affirmation`.
2. **Run the affordance gate.** From the facts given, write who will include the group and what evidence of hostility exists. Verdict: afforded; not afforded, with what must change first; or unverified, in which case collect but do not deliver. Where there is active hostility, stop and route to the specialist section; a story cannot fix exclusion and would tell people the problem is their reading of it.
3. **Plan the collection.** 6 to 12 older peers of the same group who got through the transition, a range rather than the stars; a three-question guide (what was hard in the first weeks; what did you think it meant about you; what changed, and when); consent, anonymity, who interviews. Interview, do not write. See `references/worked-example.md`.
4. **Select and edit the stories.** Keep a story if it has (a) a concrete early difficulty, (b) the doubt it raised about belonging, in the teller's words, (c) what changed and when (a person, an action, time passing), and (d) a sign the difficulty was common. Cut triumph-only stories and any story that promises something the setting does not offer. Edit for length in the teller's voice and mark every edit.
5. **Write the framing paragraph:** who was asked and what most of them said. Claim "most" only if the collection shows it; an invented norm is a documented backfire risk.
6. **Write the saying-is-believing task.** The recipient writes, or records, advice to a future entrant from their group, drawing on the stories and their own first weeks. Say the advice will be shared with the next intake, and then share it.
7. **Fix delivery.** Once, before or in the first weeks of the transition, before the first setback where possible; the format; delivered by older peers rather than an authority; a language note saying final copy is drafted natively.
8. **Write the measurement plan.** Persistence or completion at a named point (end of first term, second-semester registration) for the target group, against a comparison, pre-registered (filed before delivery). Belonging items are a delivery check only. Expected effect: small where afforded, none where not.
9. **Write the evidence statement:** US college only; replicated with conditions; no LMIC trial found; a first use here is a test.
10. **Specify the pre-test:** 5 to 10 members of the incoming group and 2 to 3 older peers read the set. Pass: recipients recognize the difficulties as theirs, no story reads as a promise the setting cannot keep, and the advice task is understood.
11. **Stop here.** The evaluation design belongs to `plan-evaluation-design`; the story collection is fieldwork the team runs before any delivery.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Invented testimonials, or the wrong tellers.** The team writes "stories" that sound right, or collects them from people who are not of the group (city students for rural entrants, men for women in a trade). The mechanism is that real older peers of the same group had the same doubt; anything else is a different intervention and, if presented as real, a deception.
- **Triumph stories.** A story of success with no early difficulty tells the recipient that others found it easy. Each story must carry the difficulty and the doubt before the change.
- **The false promise.** Delivering the set where the group is excluded or harassed asks people to distrust their own correct reading. The affordance gate exists for this; if hostility is reported, the skill stops.
- **Too late, or on repeat.** A set delivered after the exits have happened misses the moment; a set delivered every month is an untested intervention. Once, before or at the transition.
- **Measuring feelings.** A belonging scale that moves is a delivery check. Persistence or completion is the outcome.
- **The halved gap as the expectation.** The N = 92 result is the largest in the record; the large study found a conditional effect, and no LMIC trial exists. The output quotes both and promises nothing.

## When to bring in a specialist

Tell the user to consult a safeguarding lead or the institution's leadership, and a local qualitative researcher, when:
- The group reports active hostility, harassment, or discrimination in the setting; the barrier is structural, a story would gaslight, and the fix is institutional, not psychological
- The group is defined by a stigmatized or protected status (ethnicity, caste, religion, HIV status, disability); collecting and sharing stories can expose the tellers, and the interview guide needs local shaping and ethics review
- The recipients are minors or the transition follows displacement or loss; a counselor should review the stories and what asking "what did it mean about you" can bring up
- The rollout is institution-wide or national with no test; an evaluator should pre-register a test first, since the technique has no record outside US colleges

Bring them: the quotes, the affordance check with its facts, the collection guide, the candidate stories, and the measurement plan.
