# Worked example: first-year students from rural woredas at a regional university in Amhara

An illustrative run. A local organization partners with a regional public university in Amhara, Ethiopia, on first-year persistence for students from rural woredas (districts). All counts and facts below are illustrative, not findings.

**Input (condensed):** Behavior: first-year students from rural woredas register for the second semester by the registration deadline. Transition: arrival in September; the first continuous-assessment results come back in week six. Barrier (from `identify-wise-intervention-target`, 30 interviews): 19 of 30 said some version of "students from the countryside do not finish here; the city students already know how the university works", most of them after the week-six results. Group: students from rural woredas, mostly first in their family at university. Affordance facts: the student union runs a peer-mentoring scheme in which third-year students from rural woredas are paired with first-years (program fact); hostels are allocated by faculty, not by origin (university fact); the women's affairs office reports no harassment complaints by origin in the last two years, and the interviews raised none. Delivery: a 90-minute session in week two led by two third-year mentors, in Amharic, with a printed set; some students read Amharic slowly. Outcomes: registration records; the sister campus can serve as a comparison. Storytellers available: 24 third- and fourth-year students from rural woredas.

```markdown
# Belonging story set

**Target behavior:** First-year students from rural woredas register for the second semester by the registration deadline.
**Transition moment:** Arrival in September; the first continuous-assessment results return in week six, when most of the "we do not finish here" statements were made.
**Barrier evidence carried over:** 19 of 30 first-years: "students from the countryside do not finish here; the city students already know how the university works."
**Group:** Students from rural woredas, mostly first in their family at university.

## Affordance check (gate)
- **Who will include them:** Third-year peer mentors from rural woredas, paired by the student union (program fact); hostel allocation by faculty, so rural and city students share rooms (university fact).
- **Evidence of hostility or exclusion:** None reported: women's affairs office, two years of complaints; none raised in the 30 interviews. Asked of first-years and the office, not yet of mentors.
- **Verdict:** Afforded. One open point: ask the mentors whether first-years from rural woredas are mocked in the hostels.

## Story collection
- **Storytellers:** 10 of the 24 available third- and fourth-years, chosen to cover both sexes, four faculties, and a range of first-year results (two who nearly left); written consent; pseudonyms; the storyteller approves the edited version.
- **Interview guide:** What was hard in your first weeks here? What did you think it meant about you, or about students like you? What changed, and when?
- **Who interviews, when:** The organization's field officer, in Amharic, in the two weeks before the intake arrives.

## Story set (working copy; shape of the stories to be collected, in the tellers' voice; final copy in Amharic)
### Story 1: "Mulu", fourth year
In the first month I did not understand the registration system and missed a deadline. I thought: a city student would have known this; people like me are not meant for here. [Edited for length.] In week eight my mentor showed me the notice board and the online portal in ten minutes. Everyone from my woreda I later asked had missed something that first month.
### Story 2: "Dawit", third year
My week-six results were below the class average in two courses. I decided I would go home after the semester. A hostel-mate from Addis had failed one course too, which I had not expected. By the second semester my results were around the average, and two of us from the countryside were tutoring first-years.
### Story 3: "Hirut", fourth year
I did not speak in class for two months because my accent marked me out. I thought the lecturers had already decided about me. A lecturer asked me to present in week ten, and the class listened. The silence at the start was the same for the three other women from my zone.
### Story 4: "Getachew", third year
I nearly left in week seven. [Account of a failed assessment and a conversation with a mentor; edited for length at the teller's request.] It passed by the end of the first semester. When I became a mentor I found the first-years telling me exactly what I had thought.

## Framing paragraph
We asked ten students from rural woredas who are now in their third and fourth years what their first weeks were like. Nine of the ten described a moment when they thought the university was not for people like them, and all ten said it changed during the first year. [Counts to be replaced by the real collection.]

## Saying-is-believing task
"Next September a student from a woreda like yours will arrive here and, around week six, think the university is not for people like them. Write that student a short letter: what you felt in your first weeks, what it turned out to mean, and two things they should do. Your letter will be given, without your name, to next year's first-years." Students who prefer to speak record the letter with a mentor.

## Delivery
- **Timing:** Week two, before the week-six results.
- **Format and deliverer:** A 90-minute session led by two third-year mentors, with the printed Amharic set read aloud; letters written or recorded in the last 25 minutes.
- **Frequency:** Once.

## Measurement plan
- **Primary outcome:** Second-semester registration by the deadline, for first-years from rural woredas, from registration records.
- **Group and comparison:** This campus's rural-woreda first-years against the sister campus's, same intake year; the sister campus receives the set the following year.
- **Pre-registration:** Outcome, group definition, timing, and the belonging items as a delivery check only, filed before week two.
- **Expected effect:** Small, and only because the affordance check passed; no trial of this technique exists outside US colleges, so this is a test.

## Evidence statement (mandatory)
The evidence is from US colleges only. The original study (N = 92) roughly halved an achievement gap over three years; the large replication (26,911 students, 22 institutions) raised full-time first-year completion only where the group was afforded opportunities to belong. No trial in a low- or middle-income country was found. This set is a first test here, not a proven program.

## Required pre-test before launch
8 incoming first-years from rural woredas (met at registration) and 3 mentors read the Amharic set. Pass: the first-years recognize at least two of the difficulties as ones they expect or have met, no story is judged by the mentors to promise something the campus does not offer, and all 8 can say what the letter task asks.

## What to check before relying on this
- "No harassment" rests on complaints and first-year interviews; mentors have not yet been asked about the hostels.
- Story 3 concerns accent and speaking in class; check with the pre-test group that this is a difficulty rural first-years here recognize, not one specific to the teller.
```
