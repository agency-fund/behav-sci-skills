# Evidence notes: draft-tdf-interview-guide

Every item below is taken from `docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md` (section C entry for this skill, section B items B3, B14, and B16). The verification tags are the dossier's. Nothing here was added from memory; where the dossier gives no number, none is quoted.

## Anchor sources

### Atkins, L., et al. (2017). A guide to using the Theoretical Domains Framework of behaviour change to investigate implementation problems. *Implementation Science*, 12, 77. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0605-9 [verified-crossref]
- A methods guide, not an empirical study. It gives the procedure this skill follows: specify the behavior first, write one to three questions per domain, code transcripts deductively against the domains, then identify the relevant domains.
- Setting: health services implementation research; the dossier records no non-WEIRD sample.
- Numbers: none quoted in the dossier.

### Cane, J., O'Connor, D., & Michie, S. (2012). Validation of the theoretical domains framework for use in behaviour change and implementation research. *Implementation Science*, 7, 37. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-7-37 [verified-crossref]
- Refined the framework to 14 domains and validated the domain structure in a sort study (participants sorted constructs into domains).
- This is the only validation of the domain structure the dossier records; it is a single study.
- Numbers: none quoted in the dossier beyond "14 domains".

### Presseau, J., et al. (2019). Action, actor, context, target, time (AACTT): A framework for specifying behaviour. *Implementation Science*, 14, 102. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-019-0951-x [verified-crossref]
- A peer-reviewed framework for specifying a behavior precisely enough to study it. The skill uses it as a completeness check on the target behavior brief.
- No reliability data (dossier note).

### Huijg, J. M., et al. (2014). Discriminant content validity of a theoretical domains framework questionnaire for use in implementation research. *Implementation Science*, 9, 11. https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-9-11 [verified-crossref]
- Built and tested a questionnaire item bank for the TDF domains.
- Result recorded in the dossier: items for 11 of the 14 domains showed discriminant content validity; Reinforcement, Goals, and Behavioral regulation did not.
- The skill reads this as a warning that those three domains blur in practice and should not be forced apart in interview questions.
- Population and design beyond the above: not recorded in the dossier.

### Gyani, A., Tindall, K., & Gallagher, R. (2022). *Explore Field Guide: A practical tool to map and unpack behaviour*. Behavioural Insights Team. [verified-fetch: https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/BIT-Explore-Field-Guide.pdf]
- A practitioner guide. The dossier records that it contains an interview structure ("four steps of Interview"), a set of probes ("Five probes"), and a list of questions to avoid.
- The dossier does not reproduce the content of those steps or probes, so this skill cites the guide only as the source of a practitioner interview structure. The probe wording in the skill is not quoted from it.
- Not peer-reviewed; attribute to the organization, not as validated.

## Related, not cited as evidence
- UCL Centre for Behaviour Change is listed in the dossier as a practitioner source, without a URL or document. Not cited in the skill.
- TAF's internal `/behavioral-science` COM-B probe sets: the dossier marks these as internal seed content, not evidence. The skill adapts their wording for probes and says so.

## Non-WEIRD evidence
- None found in the three research reports the dossier consolidates. The labs report's characterization of the evidence base as "mostly HIC health services" is itself tagged [unverified, author to confirm] in the dossier.
- Consequence for the skill: `weird: weird-only`. The framework claims to be general, but every validation and worked example the dossier records is from UK or other high-income health services.

## Replication status (as recorded)
- Domain structure: single-study validation (Cane 2012 sort study).
- Questionnaire form: 11 of 14 domains discriminable (Huijg 2014).
- AACTT: no reliability data.
- The TDF is a classification framework, not an effect; there is no effect size to replicate or correct for publication bias.

## Boundary conditions and cautions from the dossier
- Domains overlap; the framework was developed in UK health care.
- Procedure: specify the behavior (AACTT), one to three questions per domain, deductive coding, identify relevant domains.
- Corrections or cautions for the drafter: none recorded for this entry.

## What the skill adds beyond the sources (judgment calls, not evidence)
- Labeling every question with the COM-B hypothesis it tests, so the guide is a test of `decompose-comb-barriers` output.
- One discovery question for every domain without a hypothesis.
- Running order by episode and topic rather than by domain.
- A consent placeholder rather than consent text.
- The TDF-to-COM-B mapping used in the skill's table follows the mapping published with the 14-domain refinement; the "ask about" column is the skill's own plain-language gloss.

## What to check locally
- Whether "role and identity" has a meaningful local form when the actor is not a professional (parent, farmer, trader, community member).
- Who the relevant social referents are for Social influences: spouse, in-laws, elders, peers, employer, religious leader.
- Which services, tools, costs, and interruptions make up Environmental context and resources in this setting.
- Whether the words "goal", "plan", "intend", and "confident" translate without changing meaning; use concrete substitutes if not.
- Whether the interviewer's relationship to the respondent (supervisor, mentor, peer) will shape the answers.
- Whether the behavior needs ethics review before interviews begin.
