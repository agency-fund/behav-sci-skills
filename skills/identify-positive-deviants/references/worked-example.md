# Worked example: community health agents' condom sales in Lusaka, Zambia

Illustrative only. The program, numbers, and practices are invented to show one complete run; none of it is a finding, and the practices table shows the shape of a result, not a result.

**Setting (illustrative):** a social-marketing program in eight peri-urban compounds of Lusaka employs 120 community health agents (CHAs) who sell subsidized condom packs on commission. The stock system records monthly packs sold per agent. Most agents sell under 200 packs a month; about a dozen sell over 500 a month for three consecutive months, on the same allocation, commission rate, and compound type. The behavior came from `define-key-behavior`. The team supplied the stock records, named a program officer and two senior CHAs to run the inquiry, and confirmed that condom selling is not a stigmatized activity for agents in these compounds.

```markdown
# Positive deviance inquiry: community health agents who sell most of their monthly condom allocation

**Target behavior or outcome:** Community health agents in the program's eight Lusaka compounds sell the subsidized condom packs allocated to them each month, restocking before the month ends when the allocation runs out.
**Success defined as:** at least 500 packs sold in each of the last three calendar months (illustrative threshold) verified by the program stock system (sales reconciled against stock issued and cash returned)
**Shared-constraints screen:** commission rate: standard program rate; monthly allocation: standard 600 packs; compound type: high-density peri-urban; tenure: at least six months in the program; no shop, kiosk, or bar owned by the agent or household; no motor vehicle; no second program paying for health work (anyone outside is excluded as a resource deviant)

## Deviant-selection criteria
| Criterion | Rule | How verified |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | 500 or more packs sold per month | stock system reconciliation, not the agent's own tally |
| Sustained over | each of the last three calendar months | three consecutive monthly reconciliations |
| Same constraints | all screen items above | program records for allocation, commission, and tenure; a home visit for shop, kiosk, bar, and vehicle |
| Excluded if | any screen item fails, or sales include bulk transfers to another agent | stock system transfer log; home visit |

## Candidate list
| Unit (anonymized) | Outcome by period | Constraints check | Verified by |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 to A8 | over 500 in each of months 1, 2, 3 | pass | reconciliation; home visit |
| A9, A10 | over 500 in each of months 1, 2, 3 | fail: household owns a kiosk | home visit; excluded as resource deviants |
| A11 | over 500 in each of months 1, 2, 3 | fail: has a motorbike | home visit; excluded |
| A12 | over 500 in months 2 and 3 only | not sustained | reconciliation; excluded |

## Inquiry plan
- Deviants: 8 (A1 to A8); observation of one full selling shift each, afternoon into evening, plus an interview on where they sell, at what times, to whom, how they pitch, how they carry and restock, what it costs them, and what family and neighbors say
- Matched non-deviants: 8 agents from the same compounds, same sex and tenure band, selling under 200 packs a month for the same three months; same observation and interview
- Who inquires: one program officer and two senior CHAs from other compounds | Field dates: two weeks | Consent and confidentiality: agents consent to observation; sales figures are not disclosed to other agents; the candidate list stays with the program officer
- Community verification session: 12 agents from compounds not in the inquiry; practices presented as a list without names; each rated as recognized, acceptable, and doable on a standard allocation and commission

## Practices table
(Illustrative rows showing the shape of a result; not findings.)
| Common practice | Deviant practice | What makes it feasible | Observed or reported | Bears on hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Door-to-door in daytime, when many customers are out | Sells at taverns and bus stops in the early evening | No cost; needs willingness to work evenings; safety concern for women agents | observed (shift) | Opportunity, physical: customers absent at home during the day |
| Carries the whole allocation in the program bag | Leaves small consignments with a tavern owner or hair salon, settles weekly | Needs trust and a tally sheet; small risk of loss | observed (consignment seen); settlement reported | Opportunity, physical: selling time is the limit, not demand |
| Restocks at the monthly meeting only | Keeps a float of about one week's commission to restock midweek | Needs a small cash float; feasible on the standard commission after the first month | reported, not yet observed | Capability, psychological: planning stock against demand |
| Pitches the health message | Pitches price and discretion: cheaper than the shop, nobody sees you buy | No cost; needs confidence; acceptable in the compounds visited | reported | Motivation, automatic, of the customer: embarrassment at the shop counter |

## Open questions
- The midweek float is reported by five deviants but was not observed; verify by checking restock dates in the stock system against meeting dates.
- A3 and A6 sell mainly through a consignment partner; check whether the partner is a relative with a shop, which would make them resource deviants.
- The evening-selling practice may be feasible only for men; the verification session must include women agents and ask about safety.

## What this does not show
- Whether these practices cause the higher sales, or whether spreading them would raise other agents' sales. As an intervention, positive deviance has weak or null evidence (Triatmaja et al. 2023; Bisits Bullen 2011). Testing a practice with a few agents first is plan-trials-of-improved-practices.
```
