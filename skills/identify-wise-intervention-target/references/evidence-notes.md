Every source below comes from `docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md` (compiled 2026-10-12). Verification tags are carried over as the dossier gives them; nothing has been upgraded or added from memory. Numbers appear only as the dossier writes them.

## Framework

### Walton & Wilson (2018). Wise interventions: Psychological remedies for social and personal problems [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Review*, 125(5), 617–655. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000115.
- The framework: brief, precise interventions that change a specific meaning people make of themselves or their situation, so that a recursive process can turn. The four-question reduction (belonging, ability, self-integrity, trust) is this skill's operationalization, not the paper's own list.
- The dossier records no numbers from this paper.

### Cohen & Sherman (2014). The psychology of change: Self-affirmation and social psychological intervention [verified-crossref]
*Annual Review of Psychology*, 65, 333–371. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115137.
- Review of self-affirmation as a buffer against identity threat; the source for the self-integrity question. No numbers recorded in the dossier.

## Belonging

### Walton et al. (2023). Where and with whom does a brief social-belonging intervention promote progress in college? [verified-crossref]
*Science*, 380(6644), 499–505. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade4420.
- 26,911 students at 22 institutions. Raised full-time first-year completion only where the group was afforded opportunities to belong. This is the context-affordance condition the skill makes mandatory.
- Crossref lists an erratum (10.1126/science.ads9718, 2024); the author should check what it changes before quoting the numbers in a published skill.

### Walton & Cohen (2011). A brief social-belonging intervention improves academic and health outcomes of minority students [verified-crossref]
*Science*, 331(6023), 1447–1451. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198364. (Listed in the dossier under `draft-belonging-story`.)
- Original study, N = 92; the achievement gap roughly halved over 3 years. US college.

## Self-affirmation

### Hall, Zhao & Shafir (2014). Self-affirmation among the poor [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Science*, 25(2), 619–625. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613510949. Audit year 2013 (online-first).
- US soup kitchen. Oral affirmation improved executive control and fluid intelligence and raised willingness to seek benefits; no effect among the wealthy. Supports the oral-format note where literacy is low.

### Hanselman, Rozek, Grigg & Borman (2017). Large-scale replications of self-affirmation effects [verified-crossref]
*Journal of Educational Psychology*, 109(3). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000141.
- Large-scale school replication ruled out affirmation effects larger than 0.10.

### Baranov, Haushofer & Jang (2020). Positive psychology in Kenya [verified-crossref]
*Economic Development and Cultural Change*. https://doi.org/10.1086/702860.
- Kibera, Nairobi. N = 220 adults. Affirmation bundled with gratitude and aspirations exercises. Gratitude rose 0.3 SD; no effect on wellbeing, aspirations, cognitive control, or discounting. The only non-WEIRD affirmation trial in the dossier, and null on its targeted outcomes; also the reason this skill refuses bundles.

### Protzko & Aronson (2016). Failed affirmation replication [unverified, author to confirm]
- Seen only as a search snippet in the research reports. Do not cite as established.

## Growth mindset (ability)

### Yeager et al. (2019). A national experiment reveals where a growth mindset improves achievement [verified-crossref]
*Nature*, 573, 364–369. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1466-y.
- US national study. Effects where peer norms supported the message; about +0.10 GPA among lower achievers (Education Week, secondary source per the report).

### Sisk, Burgoyne, Sun, Butler & Macnamara (2018). Two meta-analyses [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Science*, 29(4), 549–571. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617739704.
- d = 0.08 (k = 43, N = 57,155); about 0.19 for high-risk students and 0.34 for low-SES students, on limited evidence.

### Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023). Systematic review and meta-analysis [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Bulletin*, 149(3–4), 133–173. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000352.
- 94% of interventions were confounded; authors with financial incentives reported larger effects.

### Burnette et al. (2023). For whom, how, and why might such interventions work? [verified-crossref]
*Psychological Bulletin*, 149(3–4), 174–205. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000368.
- Positive effects for those expected to benefit, with large variation.

### Outes-León, Sánchez & Vakis (2020). Growth mindset in Peru [verified-crossref]
World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 9141. https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-9141.
- ¡Expande tu Mente!: 90 minutes, about $0.20 per pupil, more than 50,000 students. Math gains 0.14 SD by the World Bank's figure, 0.05 to 0.25 depending on estimator, larger outside Lima; the World Bank summary says about 0.2 SD. The figures conflict; quote the range and say so.

### Ganimian (2020). Growth-mindset interventions at scale: Argentina [verified-crossref]
*Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis*, 42(3), 417–438. https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373720938041.
- Salta, 202 schools. Null, with small effects ruled out.

### Other non-WEIRD mindset work (no results in the dossier)
- An Indonesia replication ("Semua Bisa Pintar!", about 160,000 students) and a South Africa adaptation exist per the labs report, with no results cited. Do not quote outcomes.

## Trust
- No source in the dossier addresses the trust question for this skill. The output must say so when trust is the primary question.

## Non-WEIRD evidence, summarized
- Kenya (Baranov et al. 2020): null on targeted outcomes. Peru (Outes-León et al. 2020): positive, modest, conflicting estimates. Argentina (Ganimian 2020): null. No non-WEIRD belonging evidence found in the three reports.

## Replication status, per family
- Belonging: replicated with conditions (context affordance).
- Self-affirmation: contested (replication ceiling of 0.10; one positive US adult study; Kenya null; one failed replication unverified).
- Growth mindset: contested (d = 0.08; confounding in 94% of interventions; Peru modest, Argentina null).
- Trust: no evidence in the sources this skill draws on.

## Known corrections and cautions (from the dossier)
- Walton et al. (2023) has an erratum; author to check.
- Hall et al.: print 2014, online-first 2013.
- Peru: quote the 0.05 to 0.25 range alongside 0.14 and "about 0.2"; the figures conflict.
- The Yeager 2019 GPA figure and the Peru World Bank summary figure are secondary sources per the report.
- Protzko & Aronson (2016) is unverified.
- The dossier lists no practitioner source for this skill.

## What to check locally
- Whether the four questions have a natural local phrasing; an interpretation that does not map cleanly to any of them is a signal, not a nuisance.
- Whether the setting can deliver the promise: a second attempt, inclusive peers, an institution that does not discriminate. This is the check most often skipped.
- Whether an oral, individual format is needed (literacy, group dynamics).
- Whether the local examination system is one-shot; "you can improve" needs an occasion to improve on.
- The timing of the identity threat (transition, first failure, evaluation) in this program's calendar.
