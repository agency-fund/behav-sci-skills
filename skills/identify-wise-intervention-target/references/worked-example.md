# Worked example: after-school math reinforcement in Peruvian public secondary schools

An illustrative run. The program is a regional education office in the Peruvian highlands running after-school math reinforcement sessions in 40 public secondary schools (illustrative). All counts below are illustrative, not findings.

**Behavior (from `define-key-behavior`):** "Second-year secondary students who fail the first bimester math quiz attend the after-school reinforcement session twice a week for the following six weeks."

**Barrier evidence (from `decompose-comb-barriers` and a field round):** 36 interviews with students who failed the quiz, across 6 of the 40 schools. Motivation (reflective) hypothesis supported: students read the failure as proof they are not "math people". Opportunity (physical) partly supported: harvest labor in two districts.

# Wise-intervention target

**Behavior:** Second-year secondary students who fail the first bimester math quiz attend the after-school reinforcement session twice a week for the following six weeks.
**Barrier evidence carried over:** Motivation (reflective) supported in 36 interviews (6 of 40 schools): failure read as a fixed verdict on ability. Opportunity (physical) partly supported: harvest labor, two districts.
**Audience interpretations used:** 36 student interviews, two weeks after quiz results, by the program's field team.

## How the audience reads the situation

| Interpretation (their words) | Said by (n of N) | Candidate question |
|---|---|---|
| "Math is for people with a head for numbers. I don't have one." | 21 of 36 | ability |
| "The teacher already decided which of us are the clever ones." | 12 of 36 | trust |
| "In harvest I have to be in the field after school." | 9 of 36 (two districts) | none |
| "Kids from the campo don't go on to the institute anyway." | 8 of 36 | belonging |
| "The session is the same room, the same teacher who marked me down." | 6 of 36 | trust |

## Structural test

| Interpretation | Material barrier in play | If removed, would the behavior follow? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not a math person | none | No: students with the same timetable and transport differ on this alone | meaning |
| Teacher decided who is clever | teacher conduct (a setting fact) | Partly: if the teacher's conduct changed, some would return; the reading would persist for others | both: conduct is an affordance matter; the reading is meaning |
| Harvest labor | time | Yes: a session before harvest hours, or paused in harvest, removes it | structural: to select-intervention-levers |
| Campo kids do not go on | none immediate | No: the pathway exists; the belief about who takes it is the barrier | meaning |
| Same room, same teacher | process | Yes in part: a different room or tutor would remove the cue | structural, and an affordance fact for the primary question |

## The question
**Primary:** ability: "Can I get better at this, or was the quiz the verdict?"
**Why this one, not the others:** Said by 21 of 36 across all six schools; the belonging reading (8) is concentrated in the two most rural schools and may be a separate target there; the trust reading (12) depends on teacher conduct, which is an affordance condition for the ability message rather than a separate intervention at this stage.
**When it becomes live:** The day quiz results are returned; sessions start the following week. The intervention must land between the two.

## Affordance check
**What the message would promise:** "Ability grows with practice, and the sessions are where that happens."
**What the setting actually offers:** A retake quiz at week six exists (program fact). Sessions give worked problems but no individual feedback (program fact). The sessions are run by the same teacher who marked the quiz, and 12 of 36 students say that teacher has already sorted them (interview fact). Peer norms about the sessions are not known.
**Verdict:** Partly afforded. The retake gives an occasion to improve; the absence of individual feedback and the teacher's reported sorting could contradict the message. What would have to change first: a teacher briefing so the session does not contradict the message, and some individual feedback in the sessions. Both are design matters for the hand-off.

## Replication picture for this family
Growth mindset is contested. Meta-analysis: d = 0.08 (k = 43, N = 57,155), about 0.19 for high-risk and 0.34 for low-SES students on limited evidence; a 2023 review found 94% of interventions confounded. Peru (90-minute session, more than 50,000 students): math gains reported as 0.14 SD, 0.05 to 0.25 depending on estimator, larger outside Lima, about 0.2 SD in the World Bank summary; the figures conflict. Argentina (Salta, 202 schools): null, small effects ruled out. The US national study found effects where peer norms supported the message. No effect size is promised here.

## Hand-off
`adapt-growth-mindset-message`, with the affordance conditions above as requirements. `select-intervention-levers` for the harvest-season timing and the same-room cue. Pre-register: attendance over six weeks and the week-six retake score, not stated beliefs; the lower-achieving subgroup; delivery in the week between results and the first session.

## What to check before relying on this
- The belonging reading rests on 8 students in 2 rural schools; it may be the primary question there.
- "Peer norms are not known" is the affordance fact most likely to be a hope; ask students what their friends say about attending.
