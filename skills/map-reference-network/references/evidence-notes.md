## Practitioner method

### Institute for Reproductive Health, Georgetown University (2020). *Social Norms Exploration Tool (SNET)* [verified-fetch: https://www.irh.org/social-norms-exploration/]
- The fetched page describes Phase 2, "Identify Reference Groups", as finding those who matter to individuals and the way they behave, and says the field-tested version has been applied in over 15 settings.
- The tool uses participatory exercises and "speed interviews" and has the community verify the result.
- The ALIGN platform record (dated 27 January 2020) at https://www.alignplatform.org/resources/social-norms-exploration-tool-snet was also fetched.
- Related SNET materials listed at https://www.irh.org/?s=social+norms+exploration+tool [verified-fetch, titles only, years not shown]: SNET Training Package; SNET Factsheet; "Engaging Communities in Exploring Social Norms: Learnings from the SNET".
- Caution: the labs research report names the Democratic Republic of the Congo as a pilot site; the fetched pages do not name any country. Say "15+ settings" and nothing more specific. The fetched pages also do not mention vignettes.

### UNICEF Innocenti / Penn Social Norms Group (2016). *Why Do People Do What They Do? A Social Norms Manual for Viet Nam, Indonesia and the Philippines* [unverified, author to confirm]
- Reported by the labs research report to cover three reference-network designs: full-network, snowball, and egocentric. Every UNICEF URL tried in the audit redirected or returned 404; the Penn resources page is behind a login. A Zimbabwe and Swaziland edition is also reported.

## Framework

Oxford University Press. https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190622046.001.0001. Print 2016; the Oxford Scholarship Online record says 2017.

## What to check locally
- Whether respondents will name anyone: in some settings the honest answer to "whose opinion matters" is a role ("the elders"), not a person, and the design should accept roles.
- Whether women name men freely, and whether younger respondents name older relatives in front of them; private individual interviews are the default.
- Whether "the community leader" or "the health worker" is a polite answer; the "if they changed, would you?" probe is the check.
- Whether people outside the locality (a migrant spouse, a relative in another village, an employer, a religious authority) belong in the map; they often do.
- Whether the practice is sensitive enough that recording names creates risk; if so, use roles only and route through the specialist section before collection.
