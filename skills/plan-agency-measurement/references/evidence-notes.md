# Evidence notes: plan-agency-measurement

Compiled 2026-10-12 from three literature checks run while drafting: every source linked from Wu, Goldberg & Xu (2026), the literature on measuring agency in development economics and psychology, and the literature on agency in human-AI interaction and on LLM-based measurement. Sources used only for defining agency (Sen, Kabeer, Skinner, self-determination theory, Jigsaw's dimensions, the HCI agency literature) moved to `define-agency-construct`'s evidence notes when the skill was split. Verification tags: **[verified-crossref]** means the DOI was resolved through the Crossref API and title, authors, year, and venue matched; **[verified-arxiv]** means the arXiv record was checked; **[verified-url]** means the page was opened on 2026-10-12. Anything not confirmed is marked "unverified, author to confirm". Nothing was added from memory.

## Primary sources

### Wu, Z., Goldberg, B., & Xu, R. (2026, July 1). *Measuring agency in a digital context* [verified-url]
The Agency Fund (Substack), co-developed with Google Jigsaw. https://theagencyfund.substack.com/p/measuring-agency-in-a-digital-context
- The working definition ("a person's capability to navigate toward the future they want"), the four reasons agency is hard to measure (latent, multidimensional, culturally situated, context-specific), the two distinctions behind the 2x2 agency framework (end goal versus digital tool; internally experienced versus externally expressed), on- versus off-platform data, the three steps (define, measure, validate), and the hypothetical AI-mentor table that `worked-example.md` builds on.
- Practitioner guidance from the authors' work with partners (Digital Green, Jacaranda Health, Nova Escola, Udhyam, The Apprentice Project, Kabakoo, a Kentucky civic project), not a study.

### The Agency Fund. (2026). *ask-agency-measurement* [source files read locally; the URL is the one the blog links]
https://github.com/agency-fund/taf-skills/tree/main/ask-agency-measurement. ## Agency frameworks behind the measures

- **Bandura, A. (2001).** Social cognitive theory: An agentic perspective. *Annual Review of Psychology, 52*(1), 1–26. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.52.1.1 [verified-crossref]. Intentionality, forethought, self-reactiveness, self-reflectiveness; direct, proxy, and collective agency. Theory.
- **Alkire, S. (2008).** *Concepts and measures of agency* (OPHI Working Paper No. 9). University of Oxford. https://ophi.org.uk/publication/WP-09 [verified-url]; also in K. Basu & R. Kanbur (Eds.), *Arguments for a better world*, Oxford University Press, https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199239115.003.0025 [verified-crossref; editors, volume, and pages: author to confirm]. Sen's definition; autonomy versus ability; existing measures capture control rather than effective freedom; the counterfactual follow-up to decision-making items (citing Alsop & Heinsohn); proxy agency as effective power. Conceptual review.
- **Ibrahim, S., & Alkire, S. (2007).** Agency and empowerment: A proposal for internationally comparable indicators. *Oxford Development Studies, 35*(4), 379–403. https://doi.org/10.1080/13600810701701897 [verified-crossref]. Four short indicators; item wording quoted in `measurement-options.md` is from the working paper read by OCR (author to confirm against the published article). Indicator proposal, not a validation study.
- **Donald, A., Koolwal, G., Annan, J., Falb, K., & Goldstein, M. (2020).** Measuring women's agency. *Feminist Economics, 26*(3), 200–226. https://doi.org/10.1080/13545701.2019.1683757 [verified-crossref]. Goal-setting, sense of agency, acting on goals. Empirical details (Uganda vignettes; World Values Survey control item; spouses disagreeing about who decides in about 47% of couples across 20 DHS countries) are from the 2017 World Bank working paper, https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-8148 [verified-crossref]; author to confirm they appear in the 2020 article.
- **Donald, A. A., & Hernandez-de-Benito, M. (2021, October 26).** *Measuring agency: What we know and where we go from here*. World Bank Data Blog. https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/measuring-agency-what-we-know-and-where-we-go-here [verified-url]. MAGNET's three dimensions; decision-making counts as agency only if the respondent wants to be involved; no validated agricultural self-efficacy scale for low-income countries; suggests speech-to-text plus text analysis to assess agency in women's own words.

## Corrections to the blog's sources (for the blog's authors)

- **On-platform response rates.** The blog cites Wu, Zhao & Fils-Aime (2022), *Computers in Human Behavior Reports, 7*, 100206, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2022.100206 [verified-crossref], for low response rates on on-platform surveys. That paper is a meta-analysis of online surveys in published education research, with an average response rate of 44.1%; it does not address in-app surveys. This skill does not cite it; its point that in-app checks are answered mostly by engaged users is a practitioner judgment.
- **Social desirability.** The blog's source (Brenner & DeLamater, 2016) argues that identity, not only social desirability, drives self-report bias; this skill uses its finding about directive versus nondirective questions.
- **Kizilcec et al. (2017).** "Nearly 5,000 students" is 4,831 learners, of whom about a quarter were students.
- **The GenAI evaluation playbook** is co-stewarded by The Agency Fund, IDinsight, and the Center for Global Development, after a CGD-convened working group.

## WEIRD skew, in detail

- **WEIRD evidence:** most trust, reliance, and AI-dependence work (Buçinca et al.; Lee et al.; Jian et al.; Carolus et al.); the experience-sampling and single-item evidence; the ThoughtTrace sample.
- **Non-WEIRD evidence:** the capability approach and its measures (Alkire; Ibrahim & Alkire); women's agency measurement in Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia (Donald et al.; pro-WEAI in 9 countries including Mali, Kenya, India; Jayachandran et al. in Haryana; the RAI in Chad; the J-PAL guide's examples); Laajaj & Macours in Kenya; Dhar et al. in India; Agüero & Frisancho in Peru; Bastani et al. in Turkey; Kizilcec et al. with Latin American learners; Morales-García et al. in Peru; Ashwin et al. in Bangladesh.
- **The gap:** no verified study validates an agency, trust, or dependence measure, or an in-chat micro-survey, delivered on WhatsApp or a similar channel in a low-income setting. Every in-app measure in this skill needs local cognitive testing, translation, and a benchmark against an off-platform measure.

## Replication status, in detail

- **Replicated or widely used:** the WEAI family, the RAI, measurement invariance practice, the finding that self-reported and logged use diverge (meta-analysis), the reliance and trust frameworks.
- **Single studies:** Bastani et al.; Lee et al.; Buçinca et al.; Schemmer et al.; Jayachandran et al.; Laajaj & Macours (with a Colombia replication); Kizilcec et al.; Brenner & DeLamater.
- **Preprints or working papers, not peer reviewed:** Sharma et al.; Jin et al.; Asirvatham et al.; Chatterji et al.; Pangakis et al.; Handa et al.
- **Practitioner guidance, not studies:** the blog, the AI Evaluation Playbook, the J-PAL guide.
- **Contested:** whether general self-efficacy is one construct across countries (Scholz et al. versus Villegas Barahona et al.); how far LLM coding can stand in for humans (Asirvatham et al. versus Pangakis et al. and Ashwin et al.).

## What to check locally

- Whether in-app items are read as intended in this language and on this channel (cognitive interviews).
- Whether users answer the tool's own questions differently from an enumerator's (compare a sample).
- Whether one account is one person (shared phones).
- Whether the LLM codes hold up in this language and for each subgroup the plan compares.
- Whether asking about decisions at home is safe here.
