# Research queries: finding instruments, items, and evidence (step 4 and next step 1)

This skill names candidate instruments but does not settle which one fits, because that depends on the population, language, domain, and use. When the plan needs specifics, run the searches yourself if you have web search or research tools, or hand the user paste-ready queries for their own AI assistant, literature database, or search engine. Queries get sharper when the user points the tool at their own documents (theory of change, product docs, earlier instruments) or at a curated corpus such as the NotebookLM collection released with Wu, Goldberg & Xu (2026).

## What makes a query targeted

A weak query has no context ("what is a good agency scale?"). A strong one carries four things:
1. **Construct:** the exact construct and its place on the 2x2 agency framework, in theory language where it helps ("agricultural self-efficacy, Bandura", "calibrated reliance on an AI mentor", "acting on goals").
2. **Population and setting:** who, where, what language, what constraints (literacy, shared phones, oral administration).
3. **Domain:** health, learning, livelihoods, civic, financial.
4. **Use:** what the team will do with the result (adapt one item for an in-chat check, build an off-platform battery, write LLM coding anchors, compare groups).

## Templates (fill the brackets from the plan)

- **Find the original items:** "The full item wording and response format of [instrument] ([authors, year]). Quote the items from the original paper or its supplementary materials, give the link, and say whether the instrument is free to use or licensed."
- **Find validated instruments:** "Validated scales measuring [construct] for [population] in [domain]. For each: what it measures, the validation sample and setting, number of items, response format, example items, and known limitations in [setting or language]. Cite sources."
- **Adapt for on-platform use:** "From validated measures of [construct], which items could become a one- or two-item check delivered in [WhatsApp / an app] [at this moment]? Flag items whose wording or response format may not transfer to [setting or language]. Cite sources."
- **Find the theory behind a construct:** "Theories and frameworks of [construct] for [population] in [domain]. For each: what it foregrounds, what it can and cannot detect, and its main critiques. Note any that separate agency toward a goal from agency with respect to a technology."
- **Cross-cultural cautions:** "Evidence on social desirability bias, translation effects, response styles, and norm sensitivity when measuring [construct] among [population]. Concrete design and interpretation cautions, with sources."
- **Beyond surveys:** "How has [construct] been measured from behavior, observation, conversation logs, or administrative records in [domain]? Which signals were used, and how were they validated? Cite sources."
- **Build coding anchors (LLM-as-a-measure or observation):** "How has [construct] been operationalized as observable behaviors or conversation signals? Draft candidate anchors for low, medium, and high [construct] in [domain], with the sources for each."
- **Detect erosion:** "How have studies measured dependence on, over-reliance on, or deskilling from [AI tool type] among [population]? Which behavioral signals and survey items were used, and how were they validated? Cite sources."
- **Validation methods:** "Best-practice validation for a [survey / rubric / LLM annotation] measure of [construct] in [setting], including the smallest credible step for a small team, and how to test measurement invariance across [groups or languages]. Cite sources."

## After the search

- Quote items only from a source you opened. If the paper is paywalled, write "not accessed: paywalled", cite it, and suggest the user check their library access or contact the authors. If it cannot be found, write "not found".
- Many published scales are copyrighted or licensed; note the terms where the source states them, and quote a few example items rather than a full licensed scale.
- Drafted items are labeled "drafted example, not from the literature" and never attributed to a published scale.
- Whatever a search surfaces still has to be chosen for this context and moment and tested as the plan describes. Record where each instrument came from so every claim traces to a source.
