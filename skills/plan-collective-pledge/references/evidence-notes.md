## Anchor sources

**Bicchieri, C. (2016).** *Norms in the Wild: How to Diagnose, Measure, and Change Social Norms*. Oxford University Press. https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190622046.001.0001 [verified-crossref] (Oxford Scholarship Online record 2017; print 2016). Chapters 4 and 5 per the dossier entry.
- Framework, not an effect. A social norm requires an empirical expectation, a normative expectation, and a conditional preference to conform. Chapters 4 and 5 cover norm abandonment: the change must be public and coordinated, and needs trendsetters. The skill's declaration design and first-mover table operationalize those three conditions.

**Bicchieri, C., & Mercier, H. (2014).** Norms and beliefs: How change occurs. In *The Complexity of Social Norms*. Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-05308-0_3 [verified-crossref] (plan-only citation; the dossier records no numbers)
- The argument that beliefs about others change before behavior does, which is why the event is built to revise every empirical expectation at the same moment.

**Pickering, A. J., Djebbari, H., Lopez, C., Coulibaly, M., & Alzua, M. L. (2015).** Effect of a community-led sanitation intervention on child diarrhoea and child growth in rural Mali: A cluster-randomised controlled trial. *The Lancet Global Health*, 3(11), e701–e711. https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X(15)00144-8 [verified-crossref]
- Cluster RCT of CLTS in rural Mali. No change in diarrhea; an effect on stunting (the dossier takes this summary from a Water Institute page, a secondary source per the report). Shows that a coverage effect does not guarantee the health effect the program is sold on.

**Cameron, L., Gertler, P., Shah, M., Alzua, M. L., Martinez, S., & Patil, S. (2022).** The dirty business of eliminating open defecation: The effect of village sanitation on child height from field experiments in four countries. *Journal of Development Economics*, 159, 102990. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2022.102990 [verified-crossref]
- Pools CLTS field experiments in India, Indonesia, Mali, and Tanzania. Private sanitation coverage rose 7 to 39 percentage points; child height gained about 0.3 SD once village coverage passed 50 to 75%. The source of the skill's threshold logic. The dossier corrects an earlier citation of this paper by PMC id only: it is the JDE 2022 journal version.

**UNICEF Innocenti / Penn Social Norms Group (2016).** *Why Do People Do What They Do? A Social Norms Manual for Viet Nam, Indonesia and the Philippines* (a Zimbabwe and Swaziland edition is also reported). [unverified, author to confirm]
- Every UNICEF URL tried in the audit redirected or returned 404. Named as a practitioner source only; nothing in the skill depends on it.

**Penn Center for Social Norms and Behavioral Dynamics.** Practitioner source named in the dossier without a fetched page. Not cited for any claim.

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)

- **DellaVigna, S., & Linos, E. (2022).** RCTs to scale. *Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709 [verified-crossref]. 126 RCTs, 23 million people. Academic journal studies average 8.7 percentage points (+33.4%); nudge-unit trials average 1.4 points (+8.0%). About 70% of the gap is selective publication plus low power.
- **Ferraro, P. J., & Tracy, J. D. (2022).** *Experimental Economics*, 25(5), 1441–1466. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-022-09754-x [verified-crossref]. Loss-framed incentives: pooled 0.16 SD; 0.33 SD in the lab versus 0.02 SD in the field, with publication bias among lab studies.
- **Bryan, C. J., Tipton, E., & Yeager, D. S. (2021).** *Nature Human Behaviour*, 5(8), 980–989. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01143-3 [verified-crossref]. Expect heterogeneous effects; the fetched text did not discuss WEIRD samples, so no WEIRD claim is attributed to it.
- Pattern the dossier asks every technique skill to state: published lab or academic effects should be discounted when transferred. For this skill the relevant discount is facilitation intensity: the trials had trained facilitators and follow-up; a thinner program should plan for the bottom of the 7 to 39 point coverage range.

## Known corrections

- Cameron et al. is *Journal of Development Economics* 159:102990 (2022), not a PMC-only item.
- The Mali diarrhea and stunting summary is a secondary source per the report; quote it as such.
- Bicchieri (2016): print year 2016; the Oxford Scholarship Online record says 2017.

## What to check locally

- Whether the village is the reference group, or whether a hamlet, clan, religious congregation, or market is; the event must be built on the group people actually watch.
- Whether a public declaration is a recognized form of commitment here, and who can legitimately convene one without it reading as a government order.
- What the alternative behavior costs a household, and whether a subsidy or supply chain exists; the threshold cannot be reached if most households cannot comply.
- How the community already sanctions deviance (teasing, exclusion, violence), which sets the exposure risk for dissenters and first movers.
- Whether coverage can be observed after the event (household markers, visits), since the health effects in the trials depended on coverage people could see.
