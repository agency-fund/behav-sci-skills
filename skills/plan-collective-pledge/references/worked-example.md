# Worked example: open defecation in a Central Java hamlet

Illustrative run on an invented program. Names, counts, and shares are made up to show the skill's shape; none is a finding.

**Setting.** "Desa Sehat Bersama" (illustrative), a district sanitation program in Grobogan, Central Java, working with the puskesmas (health center). The diagnosis came from `diagnose-social-norm` (a survey of 96 households plus six group discussions, illustrative); the network map from `map-reference-network`.

**Inputs supplied.** Hamlet (dusun) Krajan, 118 households; 38% have a latrine, and the rest use the irrigation canal (illustrative). Diagnosis: households estimate 9 in 10 neighbors use the canal (actual 62%); 74% expect neighbors to call a latrine "putting on airs"; 66% say privately they would switch if the others did. Network: the dusun is the reference group, not the village; people watch the dusun head, the mosque caretaker, and two women who run the arisan (rotating savings group). A pit latrine costs about Rp 650,000; the district voucher covers Rp 450,000, and a mason can build four a week. The practice is not religious; dissenters face teasing, not exclusion. The village head offers to convene in ten weeks.

```markdown
# Collective pledge event plan

**Practice to abandon:** Households in dusun Krajan defecate in the irrigation canal.
**Alternative behavior:** Every household in dusun Krajan uses a pit latrine at home, from the pledge date onward; cost to the household about Rp 200,000 after the voucher, plus labor (illustrative).
**Reference group:** Dusun Krajan (118 households), not the village; confirmed by the network map.
**Norm diagnosis:** empirical expectation: households believe 9 in 10 neighbors use the canal (actual 62%); normative expectation: 74% expect neighbors to mock a latrine as "putting on airs"; evidence: 96-household survey and six group discussions; 66% would switch if the others did.

## First movers and trendsetters
| Person or role | Why others watch them | Willing | Able to comply by the event |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dusun head | Convenes every meeting; households copy his yard | yes (stated) | yes, latrine already built |
| Mosque caretaker | Opens the prayer house; older men follow him | unknown, to ask | unknown |
| Two arisan leaders | Women decide on household spending; both are mid-canal users | one yes, one undecided | yes if voucher arrives by week 6 |
| Three canal-side households everyone passes | Their yards are visible from the path | unknown | needs mason slot in weeks 7 to 9 |

## The declaration
- Format: one meeting at the dusun hall in week 10, after Friday prayers, convened by the village head with the dusun head speaking; one hour.
- What each person sees: each household head stands when the dusun is called and places a marked stone on a board by the door; the count is read aloud at the end.
- What is said: "From today, our household uses its own latrine, and we will not laugh at any neighbor who does the same."
- Who may abstain, and how: households may stay seated; no names are read for those who do, and the count is given as a number only.

## Threshold logic
- Share needed: 75% of households (89 of 118) pledged and with a latrine built within 12 weeks of the event; the trials' growth effects appeared only above 50 to 75% coverage, and 75% is the top of that range.
- Expected count at the event: 38% already have latrines; with 66% privately willing and the voucher, the team expects 80 to 95 pledges (illustrative).
- If short: if fewer than 70 households are ready to stand (voucher not delivered, mason behind), the event is postponed four weeks, not held with a partial count.

## Follow-up visibility
- A tally board at the dusun hall updated by the dusun head after each mason completion, read at the monthly meeting for six months; a small painted marker on each completed latrine door, offered, not required.

## Ethics check
- Shaming or exposure risk: no walk to the canal, no naming of canal users, no disgust demonstration; the facilitator script is limited to the pledge wording above.
- Dissenters: stay seated without being named; teasing is the known sanction and the pledge wording asks households to stop it.
- Open items for audit-intervention-ethics: whether the door marker singles out non-completers by omission; whether the village head's presence reads as pressure.

## Measurement
- Pledge count against 89; latrine completion and reported use at 3, 6, and 12 months from puskesmas visits, for Krajan and for two non-pledging dusun as comparison; threshold crossing recorded at each visit. Child growth is measured only if coverage passes 75%.

## Open questions
- Voucher delivery date (needed by week 6); mason capacity for 50 or more latrines in 12 weeks; whether the mosque caretaker will go first.
```

**What happened next (illustrative).** The plan went to `audit-intervention-ethics` with the two open items, and to `plan-evaluation-design` for the comparison dusun. No results are reported here.
