## Anchor sources

### World Health Organization & ExpandNet (2010). *Nine steps for developing a scaling-up strategy*. WHO. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241500319
- Verification: verified-fetch. The WHO page dates the guide 20 September 2010; 41 pages; ISBN 978 92 4 150031 9; it states "The aim of this guide is to facilitate systematic planning for scaling up".
- What it says (per the dossier): the elements of a scaling-up strategy are the innovation, the user organization, the environment, the resource team, and the strategic choices. The dossier does not itemize the nine steps.
- How this skill uses it: the five elements structure "How to do it" and the output template. The planning actions in each section are described generically; the step names are not quoted. The author should check each section's wording against the guide and add the step names if the review wants them.

### Milat, A. J., et al. (2020). Intervention Scalability Assessment Tool: A decision support tool for health policy makers and implementers. *Health Research Policy and Systems*, 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12961-019-0494-2
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- What it says (per the dossier): a decision-support tool for scoring an intervention's readiness to scale; listed in this entry "for the assessment that precedes the strategy".
- How this skill uses it: the reason the skill consumes a scalability assessment and stops without one.

## Boundary conditions from the dossier
The ExpandNet elements per the report: innovation, user organization, environment, resource team, strategic choices.

## What this skill adds beyond the sources
- The rule that a strategy is not written without a scalability assessment, and that the assessment's open tests gate the pace of roll-out.
- The distinction between scaling the program and scaling the pilot organization, operationalized as a mandatory exit date for the resource team.
- "Has it asked for this, in writing" as a mandatory field for the user organization.
- An action plan in which every line names a role in an organization, with a date, a cost, and a dependency.
- The default that diversification waits until the core package is routine.
These are drafting rules for this library, not published criteria, and the reviewer may change them.

## What to check locally
- Whether a single user organization exists at all in the user's sector; in market-delivered or multi-agency programs the "user organization" may be several bodies, each needing its own section.
- When the relevant budget cycle opens and who decides what enters it.
- Whether any component needs a regulatory or legal change (who may dispense, who may counsel, who may hold data); if so, the strategy's timeline is not the team's to set.
- Whether the supervisory ratio in the delivering cadre can carry the supervision the pilot had.
- Whether others are already copying the program and what they dropped.
