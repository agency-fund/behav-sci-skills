# Worked example: an antenatal iron-folate adherence package moving from four districts to national delivery in Malawi

The program, the districts, the dates, the costs, and the Ministry's positions are illustrative; none is a reported finding. The strategy follows the five elements the dossier records for the WHO/ExpandNet guide; the planning actions are described generically.

**User's answers to Before you start.** Assessment: "scale after these tests", with two open conditions: (1) HSA-delivered counseling without NGO supervision still raises adherence, to be tested in 2 new districts over 6 months; (2) picture cards can be printed and distributed through the government supply chain. Innovation package: at the first antenatal visit a Health Surveillance Assistant (HSA) gives a 15-minute counseling session with a picture card; at 4 weeks one home visit with a pill count and a reminder calendar; core components from the adaptation record: picture-card counseling (knowledge and self-efficacy), the 4-week home visit (reminder and accountability), the pill count (feedback). User organization: the HSA cadre under district health offices; the Reproductive Health Directorate (RHD) has asked in writing for roll-out across 28 districts; HSA supervisors cover 25 to 40 HSAs each; the RHD director is the champion; the nutrition unit, which runs its own IFA supply program, is wary. Resource team: the NGO's training and supervision staff, funded for 18 more months. Environment: budget year starts in July; elections in 2 years; no legal change needed for counseling or pill counts. Optional: theory of change exists; no fidelity plan yet; nobody has copied the program.

# Scale-up strategy: Chitetezo antenatal iron-folate adherence package

**Scalability assessment carried over:** scale after these tests; open conditions: (1) unsupervised HSA counseling still raises adherence, due month 8; (2) picture cards flow through the government supply chain, due month 5
**Type of scaling:** vertical first, then horizontal in waves, because the HSA cadre will only deliver routinely once the package is in the RHD antenatal guideline, the HSA job description, and the July budget, and because wave 2 should not start before condition (1) reports

## The innovation package
Three core components: a 15-minute picture-card counseling session at the first antenatal visit (knowledge and self-efficacy), one home visit at 4 weeks (reminder and accountability), and a pill count at that visit (feedback), with a reminder calendar left behind. Adaptable: the language of the card, the day of the home visit, how the HSA records the count. Materials: picture card and calendar, now printed through the government supply chain (condition 2). Training: one day for HSAs, half a day for supervisors. Supervision: in the pilot, NGO staff observed one session per HSA per month; at scale, HSA supervisors do this with a one-page checklist. Each HSA does about 6 counseling sessions and 6 home visits a month. Dropped from the pilot: NGO data clerks; the district health office's monthly HSA report now carries the two counts.

## The user organization
- **Who delivers routinely:** the HSA cadre, supervised by HSA supervisors, reporting to district health offices, under the RHD's antenatal program
- **Has it asked for this:** yes, the RHD director, in writing, for 28 districts
- **What changes for its staff:** two new tasks per pregnant woman; two new lines on the monthly report; supervisors observe one session a month per HSA
- **Champions and opponents inside it:** the RHD director champions; the nutrition unit sees overlap with its IFA supply program and has not been consulted; district health officers vary
- **Capacity it must build:** supervisors covering 40 HSAs cannot observe monthly; districts above a 1:30 ratio get a quarterly observation schedule, written by the RHD by month 4

## The resource team
- **Who:** the NGO's training lead and two supervision officers, with the RHD's antenatal program officer as counterpart
- **Provides:** HSA and supervisor training for waves 1 and 2; the supervision checklist; the two-district test (condition 1); troubleshooting the supply-chain print run
- **Exit date:** month 16, two months before funding ends; from wave 3 the RHD's regional trainers train

## The environment
| Factor | Helps or hinders | What to do about it | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy | Helps: the antenatal guideline is under revision this year | Insert the package as an annex | RHD director |
| Budget cycle | Hinders: July start; a line missed now waits a year | Submit the card print run and training line by April | RHD planning officer |
| Supporters and opponents | Nutrition unit wary | Joint meeting by month 2; offer the pill count as a shared indicator for IFA supply | RHD director with NGO country lead |
| Elections, reorganizations, competing programs | Elections in 2 years may change the Secretary for Health; a malaria campaign uses HSAs in months 9 to 11 | Reach the guideline and budget milestones before the election year; schedule wave 2 training outside the campaign months | RHD planning officer |
| Legal or regulatory change needed | None | Confirm with the Ministry's legal office that pill counts by HSAs need no authorization | NGO country lead |

## Strategic choices
- **Pace and sequence:** wave 1 (months 1 to 8): the 2 test districts plus the 4 pilot districts under government supervision; wave 2 (months 9 to 14): 10 districts, gated on condition (1); wave 3 (months 15 to 24): the remaining 12, trained by RHD regional trainers
- **Dissemination:** the RHD presents the package at the quarterly district health officers' meeting in month 3; one-page brief per district
- **Costs and financing:** wave 1 on NGO funds (training, print run, test); wave 2 half NGO, half the July budget line; wave 3 fully on the government line; costs per wave to be set by the RHD planning officer by month 3
- **Monitoring:** counseling sessions delivered, home visits made, pill counts recorded, from the monthly HSA report; supervisor observations per quarter; indicators and sampling to design-fidelity-monitoring
- **Diversification:** none until wave 3 is routine; the nutrition unit's request to add a dietary module is logged for review at month 24
- **Spontaneous scaling:** if districts outside the waves start using the card, support them with the materials and the checklist, and add them to the monitoring, rather than ask them to stop

- That the RHD's written request binds district health officers, who control HSA time; if districts can decline, wave 2 needs district-level requests too.
