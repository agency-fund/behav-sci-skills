## Primary method sources

## What the method claims, and what the evidence supports

- The claim: letting households choose among a few feasible options, try one at home, and report back produces practices that families will actually keep, plus the modifications they make unprompted. This is a claim about acceptability and feasibility in the home, not about health or nutrition outcomes.
- What the dossier supports: TIPs is an established practitioner method with wide use in low- and middle-income country programs. No study in the dossier compares practices chosen through TIPs with practices chosen another way, and no study measures whether TIPs-chosen practices change an outcome. The replication status is therefore "practitioner method; no validation study".
- What this means for a user: a TIPs tally is evidence that a practice is doable in these homes, by these families, in this season. It is not evidence of effect, and the write-up must say so.

## Non-WEIRD evidence

The labs report lists TIPs use in Indonesia, Zambia, Malawi, Pakistan, Peru, the Dominican Republic, and Rwanda. These are report-level mentions; no program report or evaluation from any of these countries was retrieved or cited in the dossier. The method is LMIC-native, so the WEIRD question here is not "does it transfer out of rich countries" but "does it transfer out of young-child feeding", where it was developed, into other domains.

## Known corrections and cautions from the dossier

- All three primary sources are unverified by the audit. The dossier instructs drafters to cite them with the tag and keep the method description generic; this skill does.
- The dossier calls TIPs "the household-level cousin of `design-pilot-feasibility-study`". The two differ in unit (household versus program), in what they measure (acceptability and modification versus recruitment, retention, fidelity), and in the decision they feed (which practice to promote versus whether to proceed to a trial).
- The judgment calls this skill adds (a mandatory barrier link per practice, a kept / modified / dropped sheet with verbatim reasons, and a decision rule fixed before fieldwork) are the drafter's, not the sources'. They are stated as such in SKILL.md.

## What to check locally

- Who actually decides in the household (mother, grandmother, father) and whether the person who chooses the practice at visit 1 is the person who does it.
- Whether a one-week trial covers at least several occasions of the practice; adjust the period to the practice's frequency.
- Seasonality: whether the trial weeks are typical for food availability, water access, workload, and cash, since a practice kept in a slack month may be dropped in a tight one.
- Whether the program can supply what the practice needs (soap, container, potty) at scale, or only for the trial; a practice that depends on a supplied item is a different practice without it.
- Whether any practice touches medication, infant feeding under six months, or food in a food-insecure season; those need clinical or nutrition review before the trial, not after.
- Interviewer effects: whether the visitor is seen as someone the household wants to please, which inflates "kept" at visit 2.
