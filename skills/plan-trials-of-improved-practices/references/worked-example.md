# Worked example: child-feces disposal in CLTS villages, Nusa Tenggara Timur, Indonesia

An illustrative run, not a real plan. The program, villages, counts, and dates are invented to show the shape of the output. The barriers came from `decompose-comb-barriers` and field interviews; the candidate practices came from `select-intervention-levers`.

**Input summary (illustrative):** a district sanitation program following up Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) in 12 villages. Target behavior: caregivers of children under three put the child's feces into the household latrine after every defecation. Confirmed barriers: Opportunity (physical): no scoop or potty in the house, children defecate in the yard, the latrine is 20 to 40 m away; Motivation (reflective): a belief that infant feces are harmless. Candidate practices: a doorway scoop, a potty by the sleeping mat, a "latrine first" routine. Twenty households, two sanitarians, three weeks.

# Trials of Improved Practices plan

**Behavior and barrier(s):** Caregivers of children under three put the child's feces into the household latrine after every defecation. Barriers: Opportunity (physical): no scoop or potty, children defecate in the yard, latrine 20 to 40 m from the house. Motivation (reflective): infant feces believed harmless.
**Households:** 20 households with a child under three across 4 of the 12 villages (5 per village, purposive: 3 with a latrine within 20 m, 2 beyond); the mother or grandmother does the practice, the mother usually decides; visited by 2 district sanitarians in Indonesian or Dawan.
**Trial period:** 7 days (the practice happens several times a day, so a week gives each household 15 or more occasions); visit 1 in week 1, visit 2 exactly 7 days later; all 40 visits within 3 weeks.

## Practice menu
| # | Practice (who, what, when, how often) | Barrier it addresses | What the household needs | Reduced version |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Caregiver uses a scoop (cut from a jerrycan) kept by the door to carry feces from the yard to the latrine, every time | Opportunity: no tool; distance | One jerrycan scoop (supplied); a hook or shelf by the door | Scoop feces into a covered pit near the house, emptied into the latrine once a day |
| 2 | Caregiver sits the child on a plastic potty by the sleeping mat; empties it into the latrine each morning and after each use in the day | Opportunity: child defecates in the yard; distance | One potty (supplied); water to rinse | Potty used at night only; emptied each morning |
| 3 | Caregiver takes the child to the latrine on waking and after each meal ("latrine first") | Opportunity: distance (by making the trip routine); Motivation: feces treated as needing the latrine | Nothing supplied; a latrine the child can use safely (hole cover or seat) | Latrine trip on waking only |

## Visit 1 protocol
1. "Tell me about yesterday: each time your child passed stool, what happened to it?" Then look at where the child usually defecates and where the latrine is.
2. Name the gap in the household's own terms ("so the stool stays in the yard until sweeping"). Present only the options that fit: all three if the latrine is within 20 m; options 1 and 2 if it is further.
3. The caregiver chooses one, or two at most. Agree the specifics: where the scoop or potty will live, who empties it, at what times.
4. "What do you think will be hard about this?" Record in her words.

## Visit 2 protocol
1. "Did you try it? About how many times since I was here?"
2. "What did you change about it, and why?" (in her words)
3. "What did your husband, mother-in-law, or neighbors say?"
4. "Will you keep doing it? What would make it easier?"

## Recording sheet
| Household | Practice | Tried (times) | Kept / modified / dropped | Modification | Reason (verbatim) | Others' reaction | Continue? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NTT-04 (illustrative) | 1 | ~12 | modified | scoop to a covered pit, emptied daily | "The latrine is too far when he is crying" | mother-in-law approved | yes |

## Decision rule (fixed before fieldwork)
- Keep: most households that tried the practice kept it or modified it in a mechanism-preserving way, with no safety concern.
- Rewrite: the most common modification breaks the mechanism; redraft the practice around what households actually did and re-trial.
- Drop: most households dropped it, or the common reason was cost or safety.
- Modifications that preserve the mechanism (feces end up contained in the latrine or a covered pit the same day): emptying the potty once a day; using a covered pit near the house as an interim step; a different scoop. Modifications that break it: emptying the potty into the yard or a drain; the scoop used to push feces under leaves; the latrine trip replaced by burying in the open.

## What to check before relying on this
- Whether the mother or the grandmother really decides; if visit 1 is with the mother and the grandmother overrules her, "dropped" reasons will mislead.
- Whether the trial week is dry season; a practice kept now may be dropped when the yard floods.
- Whether supplied scoops and potties will be available at scale; without them, practices 1 and 2 are different practices.
