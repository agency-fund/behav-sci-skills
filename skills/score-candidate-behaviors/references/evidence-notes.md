## Anchor sources

### Kneebone, S., Smith, L., & Fielding, K. (2017). The Impact-Likelihood Matrix: A policy tool for behaviour prioritisation. *Environmental Science & Policy*, 70, 9–20. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsci.2016.11.013
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- What it says: a policy tool that scores candidate behaviors on impact, likelihood of adoption, and current penetration, so that a program can choose behaviors with high impact and realistic uptake. Developed and validated on Australian domestic water behaviors. The dossier records no numbers to quote from it.
- Scoring sources per the dossier's boundary note: expert or literature scoring of impact; audience survey for likelihood; prevalence data for penetration.
- How this skill uses it: the three scores, the quadrant reading, and the rule that likelihood comes from the audience.

### Selinske, M. J., et al. (2020). Identifying and prioritizing human behaviors that benefit biodiversity. *Conservation Science and Practice*. https://doi.org/10.1111/csp2.249
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- What it says: applies impact-and-likelihood prioritization to behaviors that benefit biodiversity. Co-authors beyond the first are given as "et al." in the dossier. No numbers recorded.
- How this skill uses it: evidence that the matrix logic has been applied outside water behaviors, within the same research community.

### BehaviourWorks Australia. *The Method*, chapter 5, "Getting ready to Deep Dive: Defining, identifying and prioritising behaviours". https://www.behaviourworksaustralia.org/resources/the-method-book
- Verification: verified-fetch at the URL above. The page gives the book's phases (Exploration, chapters 1–4; Deep Dive, chapters 5–8; Application, chapters 9–12) but no authors or year; a BehaviourWorks blog names Slattery and Kneebone per the labs report.
- What it says: sets behavior prioritization inside the Deep Dive phase and gives the working definition of a behavior, "WHO does WHAT with WHAT, WHERE and WHEN"; names AACTT as a supporting tool. BehaviourWorks has used the matrix for recycling, food waste, and bushfire safety.
- How this skill uses it: the cleaning step (step 2) and the behavior wording in the matrix.

### Presseau, J., et al. (2019). Action, actor, context, target, time (AACTT): A framework for specifying behaviour. *Implementation Science*, 14, 102. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-019-0951-x
- Verification: verified-crossref. Peer-reviewed framework; no reliability data.
- How this skill uses it: the five elements a candidate must have before it can be scored.

## Numbers
None. The dossier records no effect sizes, sample sizes, or validation statistics for the matrix.

## Non-WEIRD evidence
None found in the three research reports consolidated in the dossier. The matrix was validated on Australian domestic water behaviors; BehaviourWorks' later uses (recycling, food waste, bushfire safety) are also Australian.

## Replication status
Single-country tool; peer-reviewed; validated only in one Australian application.

## Boundary conditions and backfire
From the dossier: impact should be scored by experts or from the literature, likelihood by an audience survey, and penetration from prevalence data. No backfire is recorded. The failure modes in SKILL.md (team guesses as evidence, quick-win quadrant mistaken for progress, ignoring headroom) are this skill's authors' observations.

## Corrections and cautions from the dossier
- "INSPIRE" is not a BehaviourWorks framework; do not use the name.
- Fogg's "Focus Mapping" and *Tiny Habits* (2019) were seen only through secondary sources and are not citable from this dossier.

## What this skill adds beyond the sources
- A basis label on every score: data, audience input, or team guess.
- Penetration applied as a headroom adjustment to impact, shown in its own column.
- The plot described in words by quadrant.
- A required first-line statement when every likelihood score is a team guess.
These are drafting rules for this library, not published criteria, and the reviewer may change them.

## What to check locally
- Whether likelihood was rated by members of the audience; if by staff, treat the ranking as a hypothesis and plan the audience question.
- Whether prevalence data measure the behavior (kits used) rather than a proxy (kits distributed).
- Whether "impact" was scored against the goal the program is accountable for, not against a related health or welfare outcome.
- Whether any candidate carries safety, legal, or coercion risk that makes a likelihood score meaningless; such candidates may need removing on ethical grounds rather than scoring.
