# Worked example: sanitation behaviors in community-led total sanitation villages, East Java

The program, the candidates, and every number are illustrative. They show what a complete run looks like, not findings about any real program.

**User's answers to Before you start.** Goal: 30 of the 40 villages the district triggered under community-led total sanitation (CLTS) reach verified open-defecation-free (ODF) status by end-2027. Population: about 9,000 households in the 40 villages. Candidates: nine from a district workshop (listed in the output). Scoring basis: a baseline household survey by the district health office (n = 1,800 households, illustrative) for prevalence; the program's sanitation adviser for impact; for likelihood, stated intentions recorded at triggering sessions for latrine building only; everything else is a team guess. Constraints: the team will not pursue any behavior that involves shaming households. No audience and context brief.

```markdown
# Candidate behavior scoring

**Goal:** 30 of 40 CLTS-triggered villages in the district reach verified ODF status by end-2027.
**Population:** About 9,000 households in the 40 villages (illustrative).
**Candidates received:** 9; **scored:** 7; **sent back or removed:** 2. "Households improve hygiene" sent back: an outcome, no actor or action. "Household members report neighbors' open defecation to the village leader" removed: fails the team's no-shaming constraint.
Likelihood came from the audience for one candidate only (latrine building); every other likelihood score is a team guess, so the ranking below the first row is a hypothesis for the audience to check.

## Scoring matrix

| # | Behavior (who does what, with what, where, when) | Impact (1–5) | Basis | Likelihood (1–5) | Basis | Current prevalence | Basis | Headroom-adjusted impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Household head builds a pit latrine at the homestead within 3 months of triggering | 5 | data: ODF verification requires a latrine per household | 3 | audience input: 55% stated intent at triggering sessions | 48% of households have any latrine | data: baseline survey | 4 (52% headroom; held at 4, not 5, because the remaining households are the poorest) |
| 2 | All household members use the household latrine for every defecation, day and night | 5 | expert: adviser; ODF requires use, not ownership | 3 | team guess | 70% of latrine owners report always using it | data: baseline survey, self-report | 4 (30% headroom among owners; self-report likely overstates use) |
| 3 | Caregivers put children under 3's feces in the latrine after each event | 4 | expert: adviser; child feces count in ODF verification | 2 | team guess | 20% | data: baseline survey | 4 (80% headroom; no adjustment) |
| 4 | Household members wash hands with soap after latrine use | 3 | data: not an ODF criterion in this district's protocol | 2 | team guess | 15% (soap observed at a handwashing place) | data: baseline survey | 3 (large headroom, but impact on this goal is low) |
| 5 | Household head upgrades an existing latrine to a pour-flush slab within 6 months | 2 | expert: adviser; ODF does not require an improved latrine | 2 | team guess | 30% of latrines improved | data: baseline survey | 2 |
| 6 | Village natural leaders walk through the village monthly to check latrine use | 3 | expert: adviser; sustains use after triggering | 4 | audience input: leaders agreed at the training | 10% of villages | data: program monitoring | 3 |
| 7 | Working-age men use a latrine rather than the fields during the workday | 4 | data: baseline shows men are the main residual open-defecation group | 2 | team guess | 35% of men report never defecating in the fields | data: baseline survey, self-report | 4 (65% headroom) |

Basis labels: data = a named measured source; audience input = members of the audience were asked; team guess = nobody outside the team rated this.

## Quadrants (the plot, in words)
- **High impact, high likelihood:** latrine building (#1), on the strength of the only audience-rated likelihood; it sits near the boundary, since 3 is a middling likelihood
- **High impact, low likelihood:** consistent use (#2), child feces disposal (#3), men during the workday (#7); each has a barrier the team has not diagnosed, and each likelihood is a team guess
- **Low impact, high likelihood:** monthly leader walk-throughs (#6); a supporting behavior, easy to get, not ODF on its own
- **Low impact, low likelihood:** latrine upgrading (#5), handwashing with soap (#4, for this goal; it would score differently against a health goal); dropped for this goal

## Shortlist
1. Household head builds a pit latrine within 3 months of triggering (#1): the only candidate with audience-rated likelihood and a direct line to ODF verification; impact held at 4 because the remaining 52% are the hardest to reach.
2. All household members use the latrine for every defecation (#2): ODF fails without it even where latrines exist; likelihood is a team guess and prevalence is self-reported.
3. Working-age men use a latrine during the workday (#7): the baseline names men as the residual group; likelihood is a team guess.

## What would change this ranking
- Men's and caregivers' own likelihood ratings for #2, #3, and #7: one question each at the next monthly village meetings, or a short module in the follow-up household survey.
- Observed rather than self-reported latrine use for #2 and #7: the leader walk-throughs (#6) could collect it, which is a reason to keep #6 as a supporting activity even though it is not the target.

## Open questions
- Whether the 52% of households without a latrine are mostly in a few villages (which changes which 30 villages are reachable) or spread across all 40.
- Whether the district's ODF protocol counts shared latrines; if so, a shared-latrine candidate should be added and scored.
```

**What happened next (illustrative).** The team handed #1 to `define-key-behavior`, which stated it as who, what, when and ran APEASE, keeping #2 and #7 as the runners-up. The village-meeting questions under "What would change this ranking" were added to the next round of monitoring visits.
