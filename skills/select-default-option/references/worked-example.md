# Worked example: a payroll savings default for garment workers in Gazipur, Bangladesh

Illustrative. The program, the factories, and every number below are invented to show a complete run of the skill; none is a finding. The target behavior came from `define-key-behavior`, the barrier from `decompose-comb-barriers` confirmed in the field, and the lever brief from `select-intervention-levers`.

**Inputs supplied by the user:** three factories, about 4,200 workers paid monthly into bKash wallets; 60 interviews and a 400-worker survey found that 71% say they want to put money aside each month and 3% have opened the free savings pocket (illustrative); the lever brief selected "pre-set a savings contribution"; HR accepts any default a worker can switch off; withdrawals from the pocket are free and immediate; many workers remit on payday and about 15% repay a cooperative loan monthly (illustrative).

# Default option specification

**Target behavior:** Workers at the three partner factories in Gazipur move part of their monthly wage into the bKash savings pocket on payday, each month.
**Confirmed barrier:** Inertia at the payday moment. 71% of surveyed workers say they intend to save; 3% have opened the pocket; interviewees describe meaning to do it and the money being gone by evening (illustrative survey, n = 400). Cost and access were ruled out: the pocket is free and already inside the wallet workers use.
**Decision point:** The payday wallet credit. Today 100% of the wage lands in the spending balance; moving any of it to the pocket needs a separate action. The current default is 0% to savings, set by factory HR's payroll file.

## The default
- **Proposed default:** 5% of the monthly wage credited to the savings pocket on payday, the rest to the spending balance. The rate is the one used in the Afghanistan payroll trial and is small enough that remittances on payday are not blocked; it is a starting value for the pilot, not a finding.
- **Who receives it:** All workers on the three factories' payroll who have an active bKash wallet.
- **Who it is not for:** Workers who flag a cooperative loan repayment at onboarding (about 15%, illustrative) are shown an active choice (0%, 2%, 5%) rather than the pre-set 5%, because an extra deduction on top of a loan installment could leave them short in the same week.

## Mechanism
- **Relied on:** Effort (present bias plus the cost of deciding on payday).
- **Why this setting supports it:** Workers already intend to save, the pocket is free and known, and the failure is the separate payday action that never happens. This is the pattern Blumenstock, Callen & Ghani (2018) found in Afghanistan, where the authors traced the default effect to present bias and the cognitive cost of deciding, not endorsement.
- **What would show a different mechanism:** If enrollment stays high only among workers who trust factory HR, endorsement is at work; if workers who opt out later re-enroll and cite "it was already mine", endowment is at work. Both are checked in the harm-check interviews.

## Opt-out
- **How:** Reply STOP to the payslip SMS, or toggle in the wallet, or tell the HR desk; any one of the three takes effect the next payday.
- **Where it is shown:** On the enrollment notice before the first default payday and on every payslip SMS.
- **Cost to the person:** None. No waiting period, no form, no supervisor.

## Monitoring
- **Opt-out threshold:** If more than 25% of workers opt out within the first two paydays, the program lead reviews the default rate and the exclusion rule before the third payday; above 40%, the default is withdrawn pending redesign (both values illustrative, set before launch).
- **Harm check:** Withdrawals from the pocket within 7 days of payday, HR desk complaints, and 30 short interviews after payday two with workers who stayed in, to find anyone who went short because of the deduction.

## Ethics check
- **Serves the chooser because:** It delivers what 71% of workers say they want, with free and immediate withdrawal if they need the money.
- **Bad-intent signal:** A factory deducting wages can read as the factory taking money. The notice states that the money stays in the worker's own wallet, is withdrawable at once, and that the factory never holds it.
- **Transparency:** A notice in Bangla by SMS and on the notice board two weeks before the first default payday, plus a line on each payslip.
- **Escalation:** Sent to audit-intervention-ethics and to the program's financial-services compliance lead, because the default moves wages; the opt-out and disclosure must satisfy the regulator's consumer-protection rules before the pilot.

## Expected effect
Jachimowicz et al. (2019): d = 0.68 (95% CI 0.53–0.83) across 58 studies, I² ≈ 98%, two negative studies. The Afghanistan payroll default raised participation by 40 percentage points. The bias-corrected nudge average is about d = 0.04 (Maier et al., 2022) and nudge-unit trials run at 1.4 points against 8.7 in academic journals (DellaVigna & Linos, 2022). This program has the Afghanistan ingredients (payroll rail, present-biased savers, employer in the loop) but a distrusted-sponsor risk the Afghan setting may not have had. Plan for enrollment 10 to 20 percentage points above the 3% baseline (illustrative planning range, not a prediction), and power the pilot on the low end.

## What to check before relying on this
- That the 71% intention figure survives a question about this month, not "someday".
- That the factory, not the program, is who workers believe is deducting, and what that does to the bad-intent signal.
- Whether the 15% with cooperative loans can be identified at onboarding at all; if not, the active-choice rule has no one to apply to and the default rate should drop.
- Whether the regulator's rules allow consent by inaction for a wallet sub-account.
