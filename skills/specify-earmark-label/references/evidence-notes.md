# Evidence notes: specify-earmark-label

Every source below is taken from `docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md` (compiled 2026-10-12), with the dossier's verification tag carried over. Nothing has been added from memory, and no number appears here that the dossier does not give.

## Anchor sources

### Thaler (1999), *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making* 12(3) [verified-crossref; plan-only citation]
- https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(199909)12:3<183::AID-BDM318>3.0.CO;2-F
- The theory of mental accounting: people sort money into accounts by source and purpose and do not treat money as fungible across them. The dossier lists it as a plan-only citation (record verified, content not summarized), so the skill cites it for the mechanism and quotes no number or finding from it.
- What it supports: why a label changes spending at all.

### Dupas & Robinson (2013), *American Economic Review* 103(4), 1138–1171 [verified-crossref]
- https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.103.4.1138
- Field experiments in Kenya on health savings. A safe box raised health savings 66%. Earmarking helped only for emergencies or for people frequently "taxed" by kin. Earmarking for preventive health reduced savings in the working-paper version.
- What it supports: the separate visible pot (the box), the two conditions under which a label helps (emergencies; kin pressure), and the documented backfire (a label for a distant, non-urgent purpose ties up liquidity and lowers saving). The skill's liquidity check exists because of this result.
- The dossier does not give the base level of saving, sample sizes, or the size of the reduction in the preventive-health arm; the skill quotes only the 66% and the direction of the other results.

### Benhassine, Devoto, Duflo, Dupas & Pouliquen (2015), *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy* 7(3), 86–125 [verified-crossref]
- https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20130225 (the planning report had this DOI as unverified; the audit confirms it).
- Morocco, the Tayssir program. An unconditional cash transfer merely labeled for education produced large gains in school participation. Adding conditionality, or paying mothers, made almost no difference. Parents came to value education more.
- What it supports: the label on a program transfer (procedure step 7), and the "label versus condition" positioning against `design-incentive-scheme`.
- Correction from the dossier: the "76% dropout reduction" figure is unverified. Do not quote it. The skill says "large gains in school participation".

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)

- A4. DellaVigna & Linos (2022), *Econometrica* 90(1), 81–116, https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709 [verified-crossref]. 126 RCTs, 23 million people. Academic journal studies average 8.7 percentage points (+33.4%); nudge-unit trials average 1.4 points (+8.0%). About 70% of the gap is explained by selective publication combined with low power.
- A9. Ferraro & Tracy (2022), *Experimental Economics* 25(5), 1441–1466, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-022-09754-x [verified-crossref]. Pooled 0.16 SD; 0.33 SD in the lab versus 0.02 SD in the field, with publication bias among lab studies.
- The dossier's instruction for every technique skill: published lab or academic effects should be discounted when transferred. Both anchors here are field RCTs, so the discount is for scale and delivery (a program-paid transfer versus a box of one's own saving), not lab-to-field.

## Non-WEIRD evidence

- Kenya: Dupas & Robinson (above). Health saving; the box and the earmark.
- Morocco: Benhassine et al. (above). Schooling; the labeled transfer.

## Known corrections, gaps, and unverified items

- "76%" (Tayssir dropout) is unverified: not quoted anywhere in this skill.
- The dossier gives no effect size for the Tayssir participation gain beyond "large"; the skill does not supply one.
- The preventive-health backfire is reported from the working-paper version of Dupas & Robinson; the dossier does not say whether the published version carries it. The skill names it as a working-paper result.
- Thaler (1999) is a plan-only citation: cited for the mechanism, no content quoted.
- No meta-analysis of labeling or earmarking was found in the sources this skill draws on.
- The refusal-script mechanism ("that is the school money" as a reply to kin) is the drafter's reading of the kin-tax result, not a tested message; `draft-lever-content` pre-tests the actual wording.

## What to check locally

1. Whether a surplus exists at all after food, rent, and debt; if not, a label has nothing to organize.
2. Which vehicle the audience can actually open and see: whether the mobile-money operator offers named sub-wallets, whether a box at home is safe, whether a group pot is trusted.
3. Who in the household controls cash, who asks for money, how often, and whether a labeled pot is a credible refusal or an invitation.
4. Whether the person has any emergency buffer other than the pot; if the pot is the only buffer, soften the label or add an emergency pot.
5. Whether the target expense is urgent and dated (school fees) or distant and non-urgent (preventive health); the latter is the documented backfire condition.
6. For a program transfer: whether the label changes who is seen as entitled to it, and whether the delivering agency will say it at every touchpoint.
