## Anchor sources

### Wiltsey Stirman, S., Baumann, A. A., & Miller, C. J. (2019). The FRAME: An expanded framework for reporting adaptations and modifications to evidence-based interventions. *Implementation Science*, 14, 58. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-019-0898-y
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- What it says (per the dossier): a framework for reporting adaptations and modifications; the dossier records the coding fields as what, who, when, why, and fidelity-consistent or not. No numbers recorded.
- How this skill uses it: the modification log. The fuller field set in the log (planned or unplanned; level of delivery; nature of the change; goal and reasons) follows the framework as the drafting brief for this library describes it; the dossier does not itemize those fields. Unverified, author to confirm against the paper.

### Moore, G., et al. (2021). Adapting interventions to new contexts: The ADAPT guidance. *BMJ*, 374, n1679. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n1679
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- What it says (per the dossier): guidance for adapting interventions to new contexts; the dossier records "for planned transfer to a new context follow ADAPT" and does not itemize the steps. No numbers recorded.
- How this skill uses it: the transfer path in step 4 (state the rationale, plan the changes with stakeholders, pilot, implement and maintain). That step list is described generically and is unverified, author to confirm against the paper.

### Movsisyan, A., et al. (2019). Adapting evidence-informed complex population health interventions for new contexts: A systematic review of guidance. *Implementation Science*, 14. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-019-0956-5
- Verification: verified-crossref.
- What it says (per the dossier): a systematic review of the guidance on adapting evidence-informed interventions for new contexts. No numbers recorded.
- How this skill uses it: the reason adaptation is treated as a documented, stepwise activity rather than improvisation.

## Non-WEIRD evidence
None found in the sources this skill draws on. The dossier notes that ADAPT addresses cross-context transfer, which is the guidance's scope rather than a result from a non-WEIRD sample.

## Replication status
The sources are frameworks, so there is no effect to replicate. The dossier records the fidelity-versus-adaptation debate as the live question.

## Boundary conditions from the dossier
Code each adaptation with FRAME (what, who, when, why, fidelity-consistent or not); for planned transfer to a new context follow ADAPT.

## What this skill adds beyond the sources
- Core components listed with one mechanism per component before any modification is logged, with "provisional" for components whose mechanism the team cannot name.
- The fidelity-consistency test phrased as "would the theory of change still predict the outcome?", so that resemblance to the original is not the criterion.
- A mandatory flag for any modification that removes a core component without a recorded decision, and for any cost-driven removal whether or not it was decided.
- A versioned running log so an evaluation can name the version its data describes.
These are drafting rules for this library, not published criteria, and the reviewer may change them.

## What to check locally
- Which components carry the mechanism in this population: a messenger who is core in one setting (a peer, a nurse) may be periphery in another, and the local theory of change decides.
- Whether frontline staff and participants have already modified delivery; fidelity monitoring data and direct questions to delivery staff are the only sources for unplanned modifications.
- Whether any modification touches clinical, safety, or legal content, where a program team's fidelity judgment is not enough.
- Whether a cost-driven removal has a cheaper form of the same mechanism (a text message in place of a call) that could be tested before the component is dropped.
