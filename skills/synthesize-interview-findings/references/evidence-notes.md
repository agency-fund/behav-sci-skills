## Anchor sources

### Gale, N. K., et al. (2013). Using the framework method for the analysis of qualitative data in multi-disciplinary health research. *BMC Medical Research Methodology*, 13, 117. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-13-117 [verified-crossref]
- The method this skill runs: familiarization, coding against a frame, charting into a matrix of cases by categories, interpretation from the matrix.
- A methods paper from health services research; the dossier records no sample, effect size, or non-WEIRD application.
- Numbers: none quoted in the dossier.

### Vindrola-Padros, C., & Johnson, G. A. (2020). Rapid techniques in qualitative research: A critical review of the literature. *Qualitative Health Research*, 30(10), 1596–1604. https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732320921835 [verified-crossref]
- A review of rapid qualitative techniques and their trade-offs. The dossier's replication note for this skill is the speed-versus-depth trade-off; the skill's output states it every time.
- The specific rapid techniques the skill uses (summarizing from recordings or notes directly into a matrix) are the skill's reading of "rapid techniques"; the dossier does not itemize them.
- Numbers: none quoted in the dossier.

### Atkins, L., et al. (2017). A guide to using the Theoretical Domains Framework of behaviour change to investigate implementation problems. *Implementation Science*, 12, 77. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0605-9 [verified-crossref]
- Cited in the dossier's entry for this skill as "(TDF coding)" and in the `draft-tdf-interview-guide` entry for the procedure: specify the behavior, one to three questions per domain, deductive coding, identify relevant domains.
- The three criteria for judging domain relevance that the skill uses (frequency of a belief, conflicting beliefs, strength of belief) are not itemized in the dossier; they follow this skill's drafting instructions and are marked in the skill as to be confirmed against the paper.
- Setting: health services implementation research; no non-WEIRD sample recorded.

### Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2019). Reflecting on reflexive thematic analysis. *Qualitative Research in Sport, Exercise and Health*, 11(4), 589–597. https://doi.org/10.1080/2159676X.2019.1628806 [verified-crossref]
- The dossier's note: "the contrast: reflexive TA rejects coding-reliability metrics". The skill cites it only to say what this skill is not: it is deductive, rapid, and reports no inter-coder statistic.

### Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006). *Qualitative Research in Psychology*, 3(2), 77–101. https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa [verified-crossref]
- The dossier gives the journal, volume, pages, and DOI but no title; the skill leaves the title blank and marks it "author to confirm". Cited as the origin of thematic analysis, again as the contrast.

### Hamilton, A. B., & Finley, E. P. (2019). Qualitative methods in implementation research: An introduction. *Psychiatry Research*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2019.112516 [verified-crossref]

## Boundary conditions and cautions from the dossier
- Build a summary template keyed to domains (COM-B or TDF); summarize each transcript; fill the matrix; compare across sites.
- TDF domains overlap (also recorded in the `draft-tdf-interview-guide` entry: Huijg et al. 2014 found Reinforcement, Goals, and Behavioural regulation not discriminable in questionnaire form).
- Corrections or cautions for the drafter: none recorded for this entry.

## What the skill adds beyond the sources (judgment calls, not evidence)
- A verbatim anchor in every cell.
- Three distinct absence statuses: not mentioned, asked and denied, not asked.
- One discovery column per domain without a hypothesis, plus an "other" column.
- Verdicts as counts of interviews with quotes and named disconfirming cases, never a population percentage.
- A mandatory trade-off section naming what a fuller analysis would add.
- The three domain-relevance criteria attributed to Atkins et al. (2017), pending confirmation.

## What to check locally
- Whether anyone reading the matrix speaks the language the interviews were held in, and which cells rest on translation or notes.
- Whether the respondents were chosen in a way that makes the counts meaningful across attributes (site, sex, on-time versus late).
- Whether the guide asked about every domain, so that "not mentioned" can be told from "not asked".
- Whether any interview disclosed harm and whether the safeguarding response has happened.
- Whether the decision deadline allows full reading or only summarizing from audio and notes.
