## The guidelines

### International Test Commission (2018). ITC Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests (Second Edition) [verified-crossref]
*International Journal of Testing*, 18(2), 101–134. https://doi.org/10.1080/15305058.2017.1398166. Print year 2018; the Crossref audit year is 2017 (online-first). Crossref carries no author field; the research report credits Bartram, Berberoglu, Grégoire, Hambleton, Muñiz, and van de Vijver.
- 18 guidelines in six sections. This is the only number the dossier records for this skill.
- Scope: translating and adapting tests across languages and cultures; the professional standard this skill's committee-and-documentation procedure follows.
- The dossier does not list the six section names, so this skill does not name them.

### Beaton, D. E., et al. (2000). Guidelines for the process of cross-cultural adaptation of self-report measures [verified-crossref]
*Spine*, 25(24), 3186–3191. https://doi.org/10.1097/00007632-200012150-00014.
- A staged process for adapting self-report measures across cultures, written for clinical outcome measurement, in which back-translation is a core stage.
- The dossier records no numbers from it and does not give the number of stages; this skill does not quote one.
- Position in the back-translation debate: for.

### Sousa, V. D., & Rojjanasrirat, W. (2011). Translation, adaptation and validation of instruments or scales for use in cross-cultural health care research: A clear and user-friendly guideline [verified-crossref]
*Journal of Evaluation in Clinical Practice*, 17(2), 268–274. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2753.2010.01434.x.
- A step-by-step guideline for health care researchers covering translation, adaptation, and validation. This skill uses the translation and adaptation steps only; the validation steps belong to a separate skill and are not reproduced here.

### Harkness, J. A. (2003). TRAPD, in *Cross-Cultural Survey Methods* [unverified, author to confirm]
Harkness, van de Vijver, & Mohler (Eds.), Wiley. The dossier confirms the edited book exists (OpenLibrary record, first publish year 2002/2003) but could not confirm the TRAPD chapter attribution.
- TRAPD: Translation, Review, Adjudication, Pretest, Documentation. Several translators work independently, reviewers reconcile, an adjudicator decides, the instrument is pretested, and every decision is documented.
- Position in the back-translation debate: against, treating it as weak quality assurance; prefers committee review.
- Until the chapter is confirmed, attribute TRAPD to the cross-cultural survey methodology literature associated with Harkness rather than to a page or quotation.

## Replication status: the back-translation debate
- Not an effect; nothing to replicate. The procedure (independent forward translations, reconciliation, pretest, documentation) is common to all the guideline sources.
- Contested element: back-translation. TRAPD (Harkness) treats it as weak quality assurance; Beaton keeps it as a core stage; J-PAL recommends it. The dossier records the disagreement and no study resolving it. This skill therefore makes back-translation a documented decision rather than a default or an omission.

## What to check locally
- Which dialect or regional variety respondents speak, and whether the written standard differs from it; the committee member from the population is the check.
- Whether items will be read aloud; spoken register differs from written, and a translation that reads well may sound foreign when spoken.
- Whether each construct exists in the target culture at all; a committee that cannot agree on an equivalent has found a validation question, not a translation problem (escalate).
- Whether a published scale's owner, the funder, or the ethics board requires back-translation; this decides step 5.
- Whether scores will be compared with other language versions; if yes, every adaptation in the decision log is a comparability risk for a psychometrician to judge.
