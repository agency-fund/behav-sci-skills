Annotated bibliography for the skill. Every entry is copied from the library's evidence dossier (`docs/plan/2026-10-skill-expansion-dossier.md`, entry `write-social-norm-message`, plus sections A and B) with the dossier's verification tag. Nothing was added from memory; a number appears only as the dossier writes it. ## Anchor sources

**Schultz, P. W., Nolan, J. M., Cialdini, R. B., Goldstein, N. J., & Griskevicius, V. (2007).** The constructive, destructive, and reconstructive power of social norms. *Psychological Science*, 18(5), 429–434. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01917.x [verified-crossref]
- The mechanism and backfire paper. US households told how their energy use compared with their neighbors' moved toward the average from both sides: those above cut back, those below rose (the boomerang). Adding an injunctive cue signaling approval removed the boomerang among the low users. The dossier records no effect size for this study, so none is quoted in the skill.

**Allcott, H. (2011).** Social norms and energy conservation. *Journal of Public Economics*, 95(9–10), 1082–1095. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2011.03.003 [verified-crossref]
- The same comparison at scale through the Opower program (US utility customers). Energy use fell 2.0% on average; the top decile cut 6.3%, the bottom decile 0.3%. Read as: the effect concentrates among people furthest from the norm, on a continuous, repeated behavior. A one-off binary behavior in an LMIC program is a different object.

**Hallsworth, M., List, J. A., Metcalfe, R. D., & Vlaev, I. (2017).** The behavioralist as tax collector: Using natural field experiments to enhance tax compliance. *Journal of Public Economics*, 148, 14–31. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2017.02.003 [verified-crossref]
- UK taxpayers, natural field experiments. Letters citing the norm and the public-goods use of taxes raised payment; descriptive-norm wording beat injunctive wording. The dossier gives no magnitudes, so the skill quotes none.

**Kettle, S., Hernandez, M., Ruda, S., & Sanders, M. (2016).** Behavioral interventions in tax compliance: Evidence from Guatemala. World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 7690. https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-7690 [verified-crossref] (handle http://hdl.handle.net/10986/24530; book-chapter version 2017, https://doi.org/10.1596/978-1-4648-1120-3_ch2 [verified-crossref])
- Guatemala, 43,387 taxpayers, randomized letters. The letter citing the 64.5% who had already paid brought about US$760k in additional revenue, 36 times the letter cost; effects persisted at 12 months. The only verified non-WEIRD trial among this skill's sources.

**Bicchieri, C. (2016).** *Norms in the Wild: How to Diagnose, Measure, and Change Social Norms*. Oxford University Press. https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190622046.001.0001 [verified-crossref] (Oxford Scholarship Online record 2017; print 2016)
- Framework, not an effect. A social norm requires an empirical expectation (what others do), a normative expectation (what others think one should do), and a conditional preference to conform. The skill uses the vocabulary and the rule that a message corrects a belief; if the belief is already accurate, the message has nothing to do.

**Cialdini, R. B., & Jacobson, R. P. (2021).** Influences of social norms on climate change-related behaviors. *Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cobeha.2021.01.005 [verified-crossref] (plan-only citation given as author-year; resolved to this paper by Crossref; author to confirm it is the intended one)
- A review of norm influence on climate-related behavior. The dossier records no numbers from it; the skill cites it for the descriptive/injunctive distinction only.

**Del Carpio, L.** Property-tax norm disclosure, Lima, Peru. [unverified, author to confirm]
- Search snippet only: about 20% higher compliance than control. No citation details were located. Do not quote as a finding; the skill names it only as unverified non-WEIRD evidence.

## Cross-cutting cautions (dossier section A)

- **DellaVigna, S., & Linos, E. (2022).** RCTs to scale. *Econometrica*, 90(1), 81–116. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA18709 [verified-crossref]. 126 RCTs, 23 million people. Academic journal studies average 8.7 percentage points (+33.4%); nudge-unit trials average 1.4 points (+8.0%). About 70% of the gap is selective publication plus low power.
- **Ferraro, P. J., & Tracy, J. D. (2022).** *Experimental Economics*, 25(5), 1441–1466. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-022-09754-x [verified-crossref]. Loss-framed incentives: pooled 0.16 SD, but 0.33 SD in the lab versus 0.02 SD in the field, with publication bias among lab studies. The dossier's lab-to-field shrinkage example.
- **Mertens et al. (2022)** PNAS, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2107346118 [verified-crossref]: d = 0.45 (95% CI 0.39–0.52) across more than 200 studies, N about 2.15 million, moderate publication bias. **Maier et al. (2022)** PNAS, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2200300119 [verified-crossref]: bias-corrected average about d = 0.04. **Szaszi et al. (2022)** PNAS letter, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2200732119 [verified-crossref]: bias-adjusted range d about 0.03–0.31; the average hides heterogeneity.
- Pattern the dossier asks every technique skill to state: published lab or academic effects should be discounted when transferred to a field program.

## Practitioner sources

- Behavioural Insights Team (2014; revised and updated edition). *EAST: Four simple ways to apply behavioural insights*. [verified-fetch: https://bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/BIT-EAST.pdf]. "Make it social" is the practitioner statement of this technique; the revised edition flags studies that "have not held up to scrutiny".
- BIT tax work in Guatemala and Indonesia: Indonesia, more than 10 million taxpayers, early filing +7%, overall filing +2%. Reported without a citation; do not cite as a study.

## Do not cite

- Two pooled social-norm meta-analyses mentioned in the design report (d = 0.35 from 9 articles; a 200-study consumer meta-analysis): authors unverified. This is why the skill's replication line carries no meta-analytic number for norm messages.
- "A tax-RCT review found only half of 22 non-deterrence-message studies significant": search snippet, no citation.

## What to check locally

- Who recipients treat as "people like me": the reference group in the diagnosis, not the administrative unit the data happen to come in.
- Whether a majority claim from this sender is believed at all, and whether the sender's approval carries weight or reads as surveillance.
- Whether the true share can be computed at the reference-group level from existing records, and how old those records are.
- Whether the behavior is under-reported in the belief survey (stigma), which would make both the "true norm" and the belief gap unreliable.
- Whether above-norm and below-norm recipients can be told apart at baseline; without this the boomerang cannot be measured.
